  period. His case is discussed in *Mencius - Teng Wen Gong Xia* to illustrate the
  limits of extreme self-denial.\n\nKuang Zhang (匡章) asks Mencius: \"Is not Chen Zhongzi
  truly an upright man? Living in Wuling, he went three days without food, becoming
  so weak that he could neither hear nor see. There was a plum tree by the well, more
  than half of its fruit had been eaten by grubs. He crawled to get and eat it, and
  after three swallows, he could hear and see again.\"\n\nMencius responds: \"Among
  the scholars of Qi, I certainly consider Zhongzi a great figure. But how can he
  be called truly upright? If we completely fulfilled Zhongzi's conduct, we would
  have to become an earthworm before we could do it!\"\n\n**The Argument**: \n\nMencius
  then probes the source of Zhongzi's living conditions: \"The earthworm eats the
