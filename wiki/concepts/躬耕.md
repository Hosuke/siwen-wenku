  ## English


  The Imperial Ploughing Ceremony (躬耕帝藉 or 籍田) was one of the most important agricultural
  rites in the Chinese ritual system. Performed in the first month of spring, it demonstrated
  the ruler''s personal commitment to agriculture and served as an example to inspire
  the people. The *Monthly Ordinances* describes the ceremony as follows: "是月也，天子乃以元日祈穀於上帝。乃擇元辰，天子親載耒耜，措之於參保介之御間，帥三公九卿諸侯大夫，躬耕帝藉。天子三推，公五推，卿諸侯九推。"


  The ceremony began with a prayer for grain to the Supreme Lord (祈穀于上帝). The ruler
  then selected an auspicious day (元辰) and personally placed the plough (耒耜) upon
  his carriage. He was accompanied by the Three Lords and the Nine Ministers, who
  formed his escort. The Son of Heaven pushed the plough three times (三推), the princes
  of the realm pushed five times (五推), and the ministers and lords pushed nine times
  (九推). This graduated participation symbolized the hierarchical nature of the state
  and the principle that the ruler bore the greatest responsibility for the welfare
  of the people.
