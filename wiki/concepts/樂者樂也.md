content_legacy: '## English


  The phrase **「樂者樂也」** encapsulates a fundamental philosophical principle in Confucian
  music theory: music is essentially joy (le/yuè, 樂), and joy is the essence of music
  (yuè/lè, 樂). This proposition appears in the *Yueji* (Record of Music) chapter of
  the *Liji* (Book of Rites) and establishes the ontological connection between the
  emotional experience of joy and the artistic form of music.


  The principle distinguishes between several levels of sonic experience. Sound (聲,
  sheng) is the basic acoustic phenomenon. Musical sound (音, yin) emerges when sounds
  are organized according to pattern and aesthetic principles. True music (樂, yue/lè),
  however, requires the conjunction of organized sound with ritual context—dance movements
  (干戚羽旄), drums, shields, and plumes—which elevates mere sonic entertainment to the
  level of moral and spiritual cultivation.


  Confucian theory posits that **「人情之所不能免也」**—joy is unavoidable for human beings.
  Music, as the expression of joy, addresses this fundamental aspect of human nature.
  The *Yueji* argues that music must necessarily emerge from human emotions and that
  the emotions must find outlet through sound and movement. This is not merely a psychological
  observation but a cosmological principle: music is the natural form that human feeling
  takes when properly expressed.


  The concept also carries normative implications. Not all sounds that bring pleasure
  constitute proper music. The text distinguishes virtuous music (德音) from excessive
  music (溺音). Only when music is properly regulated by ritual propriety does it become
  truly joyful. As the text states: **「以道制欲，則樂而不亂；以欲忘道，則惑而不樂」**—when desire is controlled
  by the Way, there is joy without disorder; when desire causes one to forget the
  Way, there is confusion without joy.


  The later section of the *Yueji* featuring the music official Shi Yi (師乙) elaborates
  on how different types of music suit different temperaments, demonstrating that
  the joy of music must be properly aligned with one''s moral character and social
  position.

「樂者樂也」出自《禮記·樂記》，為儒家音樂哲學之核心命題。此語雙關「樂」字，既指音樂（yuè），亦指喜樂（lè），揭示二者之本體關聯：音樂之本质即在於引發喜悅，而真正之喜悅必形諸音樂。

《樂記》闡發此理，先論聲、音、樂三者之層次：「感於物而動，故形於聲。聲相應，故生變；變成方，謂之音；比音而樂之，及干戚羽旄，謂之樂。」聲為自然之感應，音為有序之組織，樂則必兼舞容與禮器，非徒悅耳娛心而已。

「人情之所不能免」一語，確認喜樂為人性之必然。儒家以為，音樂所以導人情之正，使哀樂皆得發抒，而不至於流蕩失節。《樂記》云：「樂者，樂也，人情之所不能免也。樂必發於聲音，形於動靜，人之道也。」此非僅述心理之事實，乃天地人物之必然道理。

然「樂」與「溺音」有別。《魏文侯》篇引子夏之言，謂鄭、宋、衛、齊之音皆「淫於色而害於德」，非真正之樂。真正之樂必以德為本，「德音之謂樂」。「以道制欲，則樂而不亂；以欲忘道，則惑而不樂」——此乃判別樂與溺音之根本標準。

夫樂者，樂也，人情之所不能免也。樂必發諸聲音，形於動靜，人道也。聲音動靜，性術之變，盡於此矣。故人不能無樂，樂不能無形。形而不為道，不能無亂。先王惡其亂，故制雅頌之聲以道之，使其聲足以樂而不流，使其文足以綸而不息，使其曲直繁省廉肉節奏，足以感動人之善心而已矣，不使放心邪氣得接焉，是先王立樂之方也。是故樂在宗廟之中，君臣上下同聽之，則莫不和敬；在族長鄉里之中，長幼同聽之，則莫不和順；在閨門之內，父子兄弟同聽之，則莫不和親。故樂者，審一以定和，比物以飾節，節奏合以成文，所以合和父子君臣，附親萬民也，是先王立樂之方也。故聽其雅頌之聲，志意得廣焉；執其干戚，習其俯仰詘信，容貌得莊焉；行其綴兆，要其節奏，行列得正焉，進退得齊焉。故樂者天地之齊，中和之紀，人情之所不能免也。
