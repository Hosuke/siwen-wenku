## English

**Mozi's Lament Over Silk Dyeing** (染絲之歎) is a philosophical anecdote from the Mozi chapter "On Being Affected by Associations" (《墨子·所染》). The story records Mozi observing a silk-dyer at work, which prompts him to sigh and articulate one of the most memorable metaphors in early Chinese philosophy: that human nature, like silk, takes on the color of whatever dye it enters.

The text states: "子墨子言見染絲者而歎，曰：染於蒼則蒼，染於黃則黃，所入者變，其色亦變。五入必，而已則爲五色矣。故染不可不愼也。" The core teaching is contained in the phrase "染不可不愼" (dyeing cannot but be cautious)—if one enters blue dye, one becomes blue; if one enters yellow dye, one becomes yellow. The color that the silk enters transforms the silk itself.

The significance of this metaphor is multi-layered:

First, it establishes the malleability of nature. Silk does not possess an inherent, immutable color; it is transformed by external inputs. By the same token, human beings are not fixed in their moral character but are shaped by the influences they encounter.

Second, the metaphor emphasizes the permanence of染 (influence/association). The text notes that five immersions produce five different colors, implying that染 accumulates and compounds over time. Early染 shapes later染, and the cumulative effect is powerful.

Third, the metaphor carries practical urgency. If silk could be re-dyed, caution would be less necessary. But once染 takes hold, the transformation is real and lasting. Hence the imperative: "染不可不愼" (one must be extremely careful about one's associations).

This metaphor deeply influenced later Chinese thought about education, friendship, and moral cultivation. The image of the child who, like silk, absorbs the colors of its environment became a staple of pedagogical discourse. In the Confucian tradition, similar ideas appear in discussions of the importance of choosing friends carefully (益者三友，損者三友) and of the transformative power of proper education.

In Mozi's own system, the metaphor supports his arguments for [[兼愛]] (universal love), [[尚賢]] (elevating the worthy), and [[尚同]] (unifying with superiors)—all of which depend on the premise that proper influences produce proper outcomes.

## 中文

**染絲之歎**者，《墨子·所染》所記之事也。墨子見染絲者而歎曰：「染於蒼則蒼，染於黃則黃，所入者變，其色亦變。五入必，而已則爲五色矣。故染不可不愼也。」

其義有三：

一則明人性之可染。絲非自有常色，入蒼則蒼，入黃則黃，猶人性非固定，善人染之則善，惡人染之則惡。此[[所染]]之義也。

二則明染之漸進而不可逆。五入而成五色，是染之積也。初染之善惡，影響後來之染法，故不可不愼。

三則明染之義在於選擇。染於善人則善，染於惡人則惡，所染者不同，所成者亦異。是以人當愼其所染。

此喻後世多有引用者，如《[[禮記]]·學記》云「玉不琢不成器，人不學不知道」，意亦相近。皆所以明教育與環境之重要也。
