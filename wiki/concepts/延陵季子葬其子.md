content_legacy: '## English


  The passage concerning Yanling Jizi''s (延陵季子) burial of his son represents one of
  the most detailed descriptions of proper funerary practice in classical Confucian
  literature. Yanling Jizi, known historically as Ji Zha (季札), was the youngest son
  of King Tailu of Wu and renowned throughout the Spring and Autumn period for his
  mastery of ritual propriety.


  During his mission to the state of Qi, Yanling Jizi received news that his eldest
  son had died. He traveled to the area between Ying and Bo (贏博之間, in modern Shandong)
  to conduct the burial himself. Confucius, upon learning of the circumstances, observed:
  "Yanling Jizi was a man well-versed in the rites of Wu. Let us go observe his burial."


  The account records several specific ritual details: the grave pit was dug deep
  enough that water would not be encountered, yet shallow enough that the earth would
  not cover the coffin entirely. The deceased was dressed in ordinary clothing appropriate
  to the season (時服). After the burial and earth-filling, the dimensions were such
  that the mound was level with the ground when covered, with its height barely visible.


  After completing the burial, Yanling Jizi bared his left shoulder and, turning to
  the right around the grave mound, thrice wailed aloud: "Bone and flesh return to
  the earth—this is destiny. But the spirit qi (魂氣) wanders without limit, wanders
  without limit!" Then he departed.


  Confucius commented: "Yanling Jizi''s observance of the rites—was this not fitting?"
  (延陵季子之於禮也，其合矣乎。)


  This passage illustrates the Confucian ideal of regulated mourning: expressing grief
  appropriately without excess, conducting burial with dignity and economy, and maintaining
  awareness that while the physical body returns to earth, the spiritual essence continues
  to exist. The description emphasizes restraint in grave construction—neither ostentatious
  nor neglectful—reflecting the "middle way" central to Confucian ritual thought.

延陵季子，即吳公子季札，為吳王壽夢之少子，以賢明知禮聞名於世。其葬子一事，見載於《禮記·檀弓下》，為後世論禮者所重。

據載，季子適齊，於其反也，長子死，葬於贏博之間。孔子聞之，曰：「延陵季子，吳之習於禮者也。往而觀其葬焉。」

其葬禮之詳情如下：掘土為坎，深不至於泉，不傷地下水脈；斂以時服，不尚華飾，順應節令。其封也，廣輪揜坎，廣輪者，東西與南北之度也；揜坎者，覆乎坎上而不使外露也；其高可隱也，隱者，藏也，言高不及尋常成人之肩，不得遠見也。

既封，左袒，右還其封且號者三。左袒者，去左袖而露左肩也，古者袒必有號，號而哭，哭而踊；右還者，自右而繞乎封外也；號者三，號而哭，哭而起，起而復號，三者三者，以至哀不可止也。號曰：「骨肉歸復于土，命也。若魂氣則無不之也，無不之也。」

孔子論之曰：「延陵季子之於禮也，其合矣乎。」合者，宜也，言其禮之得當也。

此則故事闡發儒家「節哀順變」之義。葬禮者，送死之道也，貴乎稱情而立文，不可過亦不可不及。季子之葬，坎深淺適宜，封之廣輪適度，斂以時服而不奢，既葬而號，以情赴禮，而不失之放縱，此聖人之所取也。又其言「骨肉歸土」、「魂氣無不之」，明形神之別，示生死之義，亦儒家對生死問題之重要表述。
