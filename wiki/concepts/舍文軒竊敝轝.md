content_legacy: '## English


  "The Man Who Abandons His Decorated Carriage to Steal a Broken One" is a celebrated
  metaphor employed by Mozi (子墨子) during his audience with the King of Chu (楚王). The
  parable illustrates the irrationality of aggression through a series of analogous
  scenarios: a man who possesses a beautiful decorated carriage (文軒) yet steals his
  neighbor''s broken vehicle; who has exquisite brocades (錦繡) yet covets his neighbor''s
  coarse garments; who enjoys fine grain and meat (粱肉) yet desires his neighbor''s
  bran and husks (穅糟). When the King declares such a person to be afflicted with "theft
  sickness" (竊疾), Mozi draws the parallel: Chu possesses a territory of five thousand
  square li and abundant resources, while Song has merely five hundred li with meager
  assets—yet Chu intends to attack Song. This, Mozi argues, is precisely the same
  folly as the man with the decorated carriage stealing the broken one.


  The power of this metaphor lies in its structural inversion of expected values.
  Normally, a greater power would take from a lesser one, not the reverse. By framing
  Chu''s aggression as irrational self-destruction, Mozi exposes the moral contradiction
  in the King of Chu''s policy. The metaphor also foreshadows Mozi''s subsequent demonstration
  with Gongshu Ban (公輸般), where the inventor''s elaborate offensive strategies are
  ultimately countered by Mozi''s defensive preparations. Just as the man with wealth
  covets poverty, the state with abundance seeks to seize scarcity—a fundamental misalignment
  of means and ends that Mozi systematically dismantles.


  This passage exemplifies the Mohist method of argumentation: beginning from commonly
  accepted premises (the King already judges such behavior as diseased), then applying
  those premises to the situation at hand to reveal inconsistency. The metaphor became
  one of the most frequently cited passages in subsequent discussions of just war
  theory and the ethics of interstate relations.

「舍文軒竊敝轝」乃《墨子·公輸》中墨子說楚王之名喻也。其文曰：「今有人於此，舍其文軒，鄰有敝轝而欲竊之；舍其錦繡，鄰有短褐而欲竊之；舍其粱肉，鄰有穅糟而欲竊之。」墨子設此三喻，层层递进，由物及人，以明兼愛之旨。

「文軒」者，飾以文采之車也；「敝轝」者，破敗之輿也；「短褐」者，賤者之衣也；「穅糟」者，糟糠之食也。夫舍己之所有，而竊人之所無，此非人情之常，乃癃病之疾也。楚王聞之，應聲而答：「必爲竊疾矣。」墨子遂即以其矛攻其盾：「荆之地方五千里，宋方五百里，此猶文軒之與敝轝也。」

此喻之要，在於以小見大、以淺喻深。墨子不言攻宋之非，而先設喻使楚王自斷其非，所謂「以其所學悟其所不悟」者也。《莊子·天下》論墨學之要，曰「泛愛兼利」，此喻正所以明兼愛之義者焉。荆有雲夢之富、宋無雉兔之産，荆有長松文梓、宋無長木，此皆以資源之對比，顯攻伐之悖謬。

後世論者或以爲此喻開「五十步笑百步」之先河，要之皆所以刺當世諸侯不知止足、貪得無厭之病也。
