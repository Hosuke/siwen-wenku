  The concept of **治之綱紀** (The Essentials of Governance) emerges from Wenzi''s philosophy
  as a comprehensive framework for ruling. According to this text, the ancient sages
  established governance by drawing principles from three sources: the heavens above
  (仰取象於天), the earth below (俯取度於地), and human beings in between (中取法於人). These cosmic
  principles were then translated into practical governance through five key domains.


  First, the regulation of yin and yang energies and harmony with the four seasons
  ensured cosmic alignment. Second, observation of the terrain—mountains, plains,
  marshes, and wetlands—determined appropriate economic activities for wealth creation
  and the alleviation of hunger and cold. Third, internal governance addressed human
  affairs through the establishment of rites, music, and righteous conduct to regulate
  human relationships.


  The text specifies four fundamental relational frameworks: the Five Elements (金木水火土)
  established parental filial relationships and family formation; musical intervals
  (五音清濁六律相生之數) defined the proper relationships between ruler and minister; the seasonal
  sequence (四時孟仲季之序) regulated the hierarchical distinctions between elders and juniors;
  and regional administration (列地而州之) distributed governance across territories.


  Education through the Great Learning (大學) served as the organizing principle for
  this entire system. The passage emphasizes that those who obtain the Way will prosper,
  while those who lose it will fall. Notably, the text states that no phenomenon in
  the universe escapes the pattern of tension and relaxation, expansion and contraction—only
  the sage can maintain flourishing without decline.


  The progression from music''s spiritual effects to its deterioration, from writing''s
  clarifying function to its use for deception, from hunting parks'' defensive purpose
  to their exploitation of the people, illustrates the law of decline: each institution
  carries within it the seeds of its own corruption.


  The framework concludes with a hierarchy of official positions: Three Dukes (三公)
  for the realm, Nine Ministers (九卿) for the region, Twenty-seven Senior Officials
  (二十七大夫) for the state, and Eighty-one Junior Officers (八十一元士) for the district—each
  level calibrated to the wisdom and virtue of those who occupy it.

「治之綱紀」為文子所述治國之完整綱領。其要義在於：古之聖王治天下，非憑私意，乃取法於天地人三才之道。

天文方面，聖王調和陰陽之氣，和順四時之節；地理方面，審察陵陸水澤肥墽高下之宜，以立事生財，除飢寒之患，闢疾疢之災；人事方面，制禮樂、行仁義，以治人倫。

其綱紀有四端：
一者，列金木水火土之性，以立父子之親而成家；
二者，聽五音清濁六律相生之數，以立君臣之義而成國；
三者，察四時孟仲季之序，以立長幼之節而成官；
四者，列地而州之，分國而治之。

又有大學之教以貫通之。文子明言：「得道則舉，失道則廢。」又云：「夫物未嘗有張而不弛，盛而不敗者也。唯聖人可盛而不敗。」

樂之弊始於流而不反，書之衰起於姦偽解罪，囿之用失於奪民時、罷民力，舉賢之弊生於朋黨比周、廢公趣私。此天下之常理，聖人知之，故能預為之制。
