content_legacy: '## English


  The Chapter on Black Robes presents a striking threefold metaphor of "drowning"
  that illustrates the different vulnerabilities of each social stratum: "小人溺於水，君子溺於口，大人溺於民，皆在其所褻也。"


  The passage explains each form of drowning:

  - **Water drowns the small person** (小人): Water is close to people and easy to approach,
  but also easy to drown in. The common person, lacking discernment, is drawn to physical
  pleasures and dangerous situations as water''s familiarity lures one to death.

  - **Words drown the gentleman** (君子): Speech is "costly and troublesome" (口費而煩),
  easy to emit but difficult to retract, easy to speak but hard to regret. The cultivated
  person, who values verbal expression and rhetorical skill, becomes vulnerable through
  his own eloquence.

  - **The people drown the great person** (大人): The commoners have "blocked hearts
  and base intentions" (民閉於人有鄙心), yet they should be respected, not slighted. The
  ruler who ignores or despises his people invites his own destruction through their
  collective power.


  The passage warns: "故君子不可以不慎也" (Therefore the superior person must be cautious).
  This connects to quotations from the Book of Documents (《大甲》《兌命》《尹誥》) about the
  dangers of careless speech and the importance of self-examination.


  The triple drowning thus represents a graduated hierarchy of peril corresponding
  to one''s social position and moral cultivation, serving as a warning about the
  unique dangers facing each level of society.

《禮記·緇衣》載孔子之言，以溺水為喻，闡三等之危：「小人溺於水，君子溺於口，大人溺於民，皆在其所褻也。」

夫水，近人而溺人，德易狎而難親，易以溺人：此小人之危機也。口費而煩，易出難悔，易以溺人：此君子之危機也。民閉於人而有鄙心，可敬不可慢，易以溺人：此大人之危機也。

蓋世人各有其所忽、所易、所褻者。小人忽於水，不知其危；君子忽於言，不知其失；大人忽於民，不知其墜。是以聖人戒之曰：「故君子不可以不慎也。」

此三溺之說，見於《大甲》《兌命》《尹誥》之引，皆所以垂誡後世，示人當戒慎其所易忽者焉。
