content_legacy: '## English


  The *Liji* (Book of Rites) prescribes specific protocols for requesting an audience
  with one''s social superiors. When seeking introduction to a gentleman (*君子*) of
  elevated status, one should employ the formal address: 「某固愿聞名於將命者」—expressing a
  humble desire to make one''s name known to the one who conveys messages. One must
  not presume to dominate the conversation by steering toward one''s own concerns
  (*不得階主*). For encounters with equals, the expression 「某固愿見」 suffices. When meeting
  someone rarely seen, one simply states 「聞名」; for frequent meetings, one says 「朝夕」.
  A blind person (*瞽*) announces himself as 「聞名」. One visiting someone in mourning
  says 「比」. A youth (*童子*) reports himself as 「聽事」. When attending upon a deceased
  minister of state, one says 「聽役於司徒」. Upon a ruler''s departure, if a subject must
  deliver gold, jade, or treasure, he declares: 「致馬資於有司」; to an equal, one says 「贈從者」.
  When a subject offers mourning garments to the ruler, the phrase is 「致廢衣於賈人」; to
  an equal, 「襚」. Brothers and closer kin do not use the *襚* form. For delivering funeral
  offerings, one says 「納甸於有司」.


  This passage establishes a hierarchical system of verbal propriety governing initial
  encounters, reflecting the Confucian emphasis on maintaining appropriate social
  distinctions through language.

《禮記·少儀》詳定初見尊長之禮儀程序。初次求見君子，當稱「某固愿聞名於將命者」，表達卑微之願，望己之名得達於傳命之人。辭令之中，不可擅自攀附主題，是謂「不得階主」。與敵體之人相見，則稱「某固愿見」。罕見之人，云「聞名」；亟見之人，云「朝夕」。瞽者自稱「聞名」。適有喪事者，云「比」。童子則稱「聽事」。適公卿之喪，云「聽役於司徒」。君將適他，臣若致金玉貨貝於君，則曰「致馬資於有司」；敵者曰「贈從者」。臣致禭於君，則曰「致廢衣於賈人」；敵者曰「襚」。親者兄弟不以此襚禮進。臣為君喪納貨貝，則曰「納甸於有司」。

此章系統闡述初次拜會之禮，體現儒家以言語維持倫序之分別意識。
