content_legacy: '## English


  This passage from *Mozi·Lu Wen* presents Mozi''s philosophy of technology through
  the famous exchange with Gongshongzi (公輸子). Gongshongzi had crafted a wooden bird
  (鵲) that flew for three days upon completion, and he regarded this as his ultimate
  feat of craftsmanship. When he showed it to Mozi, expecting admiration, Mozi responded
  that his own creation—a wooden cart linchpin (車轄) weighing merely three cun and
  bearing fifty dan—was far more admirable. Mozi articulated the fundamental principle:
  "What benefits people is called clever; what does not benefit people is called clumsy."


  This statement crystallizes Mohist utilitarianism in craft and technology. For Mozi,
  the value of any invention lies not in its aesthetic wonder or technical difficulty
  but in its service to human welfare. Gongshongzi''s bird, however ingeniously constructed,
  served no practical purpose; Mozi''s linchpin, though seemingly mundane, ensured
  safe transportation for countless travelers. This passage establishes a criterion
  for evaluating all human art and labor: utility determines worth, and true cleverness
  serves others.

《墨子·魯問》載公輸子削竹木以為鵲，成而飛之，三日不下，公輸子自以為至巧。子墨子謂公輸子曰：「子之為鵲也，不如翟之為車轄，須臾斵三寸之木，而任五十石之重。」又曰：「故所為巧，利於人謂之巧，不利於人謂之拙。」

此節闡明墨學之功利觀念。墨子非以奇技淫巧為貴，乃以利人為準則。公輸子之鵲，飛三日不下，誠可稱巧，然無益於人；墨子之車轄，三寸木耳，能任五十石之重，便利天下之行旅，此方為真正的「巧」。[[墨學]]以「興天下之利，除天下之害」為宗旨，技術之價值不在眩人耳目，而在裨益生民。此[[功利]]之辨，實墨學區別他家之要旨也。
