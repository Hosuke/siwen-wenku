  ## English


  "Law Does Not Come from Heaven" articulates the philosophical origin of law according
  to the *Wenzi*. When asked "Where does law arise?" the text responds with a profound
  thesis: law originates from righteousness (義), righteousness arises from what is
  universally suitable for the people, and that which is universally suitable conforms
  to the human heart. This constitutes the essential principle of governance.


  The *Wenzi* explicitly states that law does not descend from Heaven nor emerge from
  the Earth—it arises within human society and, upon reflection, corrects itself.
  One who genuinely comprehends the root will not be confused by the branches; one
  who knows the essentials will not be perplexed by doubts. Having virtue oneself,
  one does not condemn others'' faults; lacking virtue oneself, one does to demand
  achievement from those who are established. Those below should not be disregarded
  by those above; what is forbidden to the people should not be practiced by the ruler.


  Therefore, the ruler''s establishment of law must begin with self-examination: "When
  the prohibitions overcome the ruler himself, the mandates will be implemented among
  the people." Law is the standard of the world and the measure of the ruler. Once
  established, those who comply with the standard are rewarded, those who deviate
  are punished. Even the honored and noble receive no lighter punishment, nor do the
  base and humble receive heavier penalties. Those who violate the law, though virtuous,
  are inevitably punished; those who follow the standard, though unworthy, are not
  condemned. Thus, public justice is implemented and selfish desires are blocked.


  The text further distinguishes three institutions: the appointment of officials
  exists to restrain the people and prevent them from acting arbitrarily; the establishment
  of the ruler exists to control officials and prevent them from acting independently;
  law and regulations exist to restrict the ruler and prevent arbitrary decisions.
  When no one acts arbitrarily, the Way prevails and order is achieved, resulting
  in a return to simplicity and non-action—which means not inaction, but rather self-originated
  action.

文子問曰：「法安所生？」老子曰：「法生於義，義生於眾適，眾適合乎人心，此治之要也。」法非從天下也，非從地出也，發乎人間，反己自正。誠達其本，不亂於末；知其要，不惑於疑；有諸己，不非於人；無諸己，不責於所立。故人主之制法也，先以自為檢式，故禁勝於身，即令行於民。
