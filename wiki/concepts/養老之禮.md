  ## English


  The "Rites for the Elderly" (養老之禮) constitutes one of the most elaborate provisions
  in the *Nei Ze*, establishing a graduated system of respect, material support, and
  ceremonial honor for the aging across different life stages and social strata.


  The text traces the historical development of elderly care: "有虞氏以燕禮，夏後氏以饗禮，殷人以食禮，周人修而兼用之。"
  Each dynasty developed its own approach—the Yu clan used banquet rites (*燕禮*), the
  Xia used ceremonial feasting (*饗禮*), the Shang emphasized food rites (*食禮*), while
  the Zhou synthesized all three. This historical overview demonstrates that honoring
  the elderly was a core Confucian value with deep roots in Chinese civilization.


  The graduated provisions for aging are remarkable in their specificity:


  **Age 50**: Food provisions differ from the young (五十異粻)—those aged fifty receive
  special grains.


  **Age 60**: Meat is kept ready at all times (六十宿肉)—the elderly must have meat available
  daily.


  **Age 70**: Multiple dishes are prepared (七十二膳)—a substantial daily allowance of
  prepared foods.


  **Age 80**: Rare foods become regular provisions (八十常珍)— delicacies become standard.


  **Age 90**: Food and drink may be taken wherever one roams (九十飲食不違寢，膳飲從於游可也)—dietary
  restrictions are lifted entirely.


  **Age 60**: At this age, ceremonial vessels (*歲制*) begin to be prepared—anticipating
  eventual death.


  **Age 70**: Seasonal preparations (*時制*) are made—coffins and grave goods are readied.


  **Age 80**: Monthly preparations (*月制*) are undertaken.


  **Age 90**: Daily maintenance (*日修*) of burial garments continues until death.


  The physical decline is addressed with equal care:


  **Age 50**: First signs of aging appear (五十始衰).


  **Age 60**: Without meat, one cannot be satisfied (六十非肉不飽).


  **Age 70**: Without silk, one cannot be warm (七十非帛不暖).


  **Age 80**: Without human warmth (from attendants), one cannot be warm (八十非人不暖).


  **Age 90**: Even with attendants, warmth becomes difficult (九十雖得人不暖矣).


  The institutional provisions include杖 (walking sticks) at progressively larger jurisdictions:
  the family at sixty, the township at sixty, the domain at seventy, the capital at
  eighty, and the royal court at ninety. At ninety, the Son of Heaven personally inquires
  after their needs with gifts.

孝子之養老也，樂其心不違其志，樂其耳目，安其寢處，以其飲食忠養之。

父母之所愛亦愛之，父母之所敬亦敬之，至於犬馬盡然，而況於人乎！

凡養老：有虞氏以燕禮，夏後氏以饗禮，殷人以食禮，周人修而兼用之。凡五十養於鄉，六十養於國，七十養於學，達於諸侯。

五十始衰，六十非肉不飽，七十非帛不暖，八十非人不暖。五十杖於家，六十杖於鄉，七十杖於國，八十杖於朝。九十者天子欲有問焉，則就其室以珍從。
