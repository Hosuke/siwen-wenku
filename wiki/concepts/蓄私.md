content_legacy: '## English


  "蓄私" (Xù Sī), meaning "Keeping Private [Concubines]," refers to the practice of
  rulers maintaining large numbers of concubines. In the *Mozi* chapter "Cí Guò" (Rejecting
  Excesses), this constitutes the fifth and final domain of extravagance subject to
  Mozi''s critique.


  Mozi''s argument proceeds from both natural principle and practical consequence.
  From the perspective of **天壤之情** (the natural order of Heaven and Earth), the existence
  of male and female, like the distinction of yin and yang, is a fundamental reality
  that even the greatest sages cannot alter. The ancient sage-kings understood this
  and "蓄私不以傷行" — maintained private relationships without harming social order — while
  ensuring through their restraint that "宮無拘女，故天下無寡夫；內無拘女，外無寡夫，故天下之民眾."


  The modern rulers, by contrast, "大國拘女累千，小國累百" (major states have seized and confined
  thousands of women, minor states hundreds), resulting in "男女失時，故民少" — men and women
  miss the proper age for marriage, leading to population decline. Mozi treats this
  not merely as a moral failing but as a failure of statecraft, for a ruler who desires
  a numerous populace yet harms the conditions for population growth contradicts himself.


  The conclusion holds that "當蓄私不可不節" — regarding the keeping of concubines, one must
  not fail to exercise restraint.


  See also: [[節用]], [[辭過]], [[昏禮]]

蓄私者，君主廣置妾媵之謂也。《墨子·辭過》述此事為五過之終。

墨子之論，分天道、人情二世。就天道言，天地之閒，「陰陽之和，莫不有也，雖至聖不能更」，人情則有男女之別，「雖上世至聖，必蓄私不以傷行」。古代聖王「宮無拘女，故天下無寡夫；內無拘女，外無寡夫，故天下之民眾」，以其有度也。

當世之王則不然。「大國拘女累千，小國累百」，搜刮女子以充後宮，「是以天下之男多寡無妻，女多拘無夫」，遂致「男女失時，故民少」。墨子以為，君主欲民之眾而惡其寡，卻行蓄私無度之事，此悖於理而害於政也。

故斷言：「君實欲民之眾而惡其寡，當蓄私不可不節。」
