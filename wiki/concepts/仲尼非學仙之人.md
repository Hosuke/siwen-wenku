content_legacy: '## English


  Ge Hong addresses why Confucius, despite personally meeting Laozi, did not learn
  Daoist immortality practices. He argues this demonstrates that each person has their
  natural destiny (所稟之有自然之命) and inherent nature (不易之性).


  Confucius understood Laozi''s profound mysteries and valued his teachings, yet could
  not "draw from the clear void" or comprehend the great origin beyond form. His concerns
  were limited to worldly governance, and his heart was devoted entirely to teaching
  and transformation—he had no capacity for the arts of immortality.


  Ge Hong quotes Laozi''s warning to Confucius: "A good merchant hides his treasures
  deeply as if empty; a gentleman of great virtue appears foolish. Remove your proud
  qi and excessive desires, your demeanor and wild ambitions—these will not benefit
  your person." From this, Ge Hong concludes that Confucius was incapable of escaping
  common sentiments and was not a practitioner of immortality.


  Confucius''s tireless efforts to correct the age and his concern for the world''s
  fate show he could never abandon his worldly accomplishments to pursue the "迂闊"
  (impractical) path of cultivating life.

抱朴子釋仲尼親見老氏而不從學道之故，以為此足證「所稟有自然之命，所尚有不易之性」。仲尼雖知老氏玄妙貴異，然不能挹酌清虛，本源大宗，出乎無形之外，入乎至道之內。其所諮受，止於民間之事，安能請求仙法耶？

蓋仲尼之心汲汲，專於教化，不存乎方術。雖聖於世事，而非能沈靜玄默，自守無為。故老子戒之曰：「良賈深藏若虛，君子盛德若愚，去子之驕氣與多欲，態色與淫志，是無益於子之身。」抱朴子據此以為，足以知仲尼不免於俗情，非學仙之人。

仲尼栖栖遑遑，務在匡時，仰悲鳳鳴，俯歎匏瓜，沽之恐不售，愾慨思執鞭，終不肯舍經世之功業，而修養生之迂闊。此固氣稟之異，非智力所能強也。
