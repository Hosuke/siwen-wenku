content_legacy: "## English\n\n**非攻** (Condemning Aggressive War, also translated
  as \"Against Aggression\" or \"Condemning War\") is a core doctrine in Mozi's philosophy,
  articulating the Mohist opposition to offensive warfare between states. This concept
  forms one of the major pillars of Mohist thought, alongside [[兼愛]] (Universal Love)
  and [[節用]] (Frugality).\n\n### Logical Foundation\n\nMozi's argument against aggressive
  war proceeds through a careful escalation of wrongs. He demonstrates that people
  universally condemn minor injuries to others:\n\n1. **Stealing fruits from a garden**
  (入人園圃竊其桃李) — everyone condemns this as unjust\n2. **Stealing dogs, pigs, or chickens**
  (攘人犬豕雞豚) — condemned even more strongly\n3. **Taking horses and cattle from pens**
  (取人馬牛) — condemned as greater injustice\n4. **Killing the innocent and seizing their
  clothing and weapons** (殺不辜人扡其衣裘取戈劍) — the gravest offense\n\nFrom this graduated
  scale, Mozi establishes that harming others is unjust, and the greater the harm,
  the greater the injustice.\n\n### The Central Contradiction\n\nThe core of 非攻 lies
  in exposing the contradiction of \"gentlemen of the world\" who:\n\n- Know that
  killing one person constitutes an unjust crime deserving death\n- Yet praise and
  celebrate the unjust killing of thousands in war\n\n> \"殺一人謂之不義，必有一死罪矣。若以此說往，殺十人十重不義，必有十死罪矣；殺百人百重不義，必有百死罪矣。\"\n\nThis
  logical extension reveals that waging war on another state multiplies the crime
  proportionally. The scale of destruction does not diminish guilt but increases it.\n\n###
  The Epistemological Problem\n\nMozi identifies a fundamental confusion among the
  ruling class: they can distinguish right from wrong in minor matters but fail to
  apply this discernment to major political and military decisions. The text concludes:\n\n>
  \"是以知天下之君子也，辯義與不義之亂也。\"\n\nThis \"confusion between righteousness and unrighteousness\"
  among the gentlemen of the world demonstrates that the condemnation of aggressive
  war requires not merely moral feeling but consistent moral reasoning across all
  scales of action.\n\n### Mohist Context\n\n非攻 complements the Mohist doctrine of
  [[兼愛]] — if one truly loves all people universally, one cannot condone the wholesale
  slaughter of foreign populations for the benefit of one's own state. The doctrine
  also aligns with [[天志]] (Will of Heaven) and [[天鬼之罰]] (Heavenly Punishment through
  Spirits and Ghosts), as aggressive warmongers invite divine retribution.\n\n###

墨子《非攻下》篇續論非攻之義，較前篇更為詳明。墨子以為，天下之所譽善者，必其說有合於三利——上中天之利，中中鬼之利，下中人之利。今之王公大人、天下諸侯，興師伐罪，入人之國，芟刈禾稼，斬伐樹木，墮毀城郭，攘殺牲牷，燔潰祖廟，勁殺萬民，覆其老弱，遷其重器。此實兼國覆軍、賊虐萬民之舉，非特下不中人之力，抑且上不中天之利、中不中鬼之利矣。

  墨子又辨「攻」與「誅」之別。世俗之君詰難墨子曰：「昔者禹征有苗，湯伐桀，武王伐紂，此皆立為聖王，是何故也？」墨子答曰：「彼非所謂攻，謂誅也。」蓋禹湯文武之征討，乃天命所使，誅罰暴君，非私意攻伐之可比。三苗大亂，天命殛之；夏德大亂，天命湯誅之；紂之無道，天命周文王伐之。此皆天吏之誅，而非諸侯之私戰也。

  是故墨子終篇云：「當若繁為攻伐，此實天下之巨害也。今欲為仁義，求為上士，尚欲中聖王之道，下欲中國家百姓之利，故當若非攻之為說，而將不可不察者此也。」

  ### 攻伐之害

  攻伐之害有數端：一則害天之利——殺天民、剝振神之位、傾覆社稷；二則害鬼之利——滅鬼神之主，廢滅先王；三則害人之利——耗費不可勝數，生民轉死溝壑。墨子又言：「是虧不足而重有餘也。」——以不足之地、人力，爭有餘之土地，實為倒置。

  ### 聖王之誅

  聖王之征伐與尋常攻戰有別：
  - **聖王之誅**：順天之意，奉天之罰，誅暴君、安萬民
  - **世俗之攻**：爭地之故，反相賊害，損人不利己

  墨子以為，聖王之所以有天下，乃因「順天鬼百姓之利」；而當世之君則不然，故天下滔滔，戰伐不息。
