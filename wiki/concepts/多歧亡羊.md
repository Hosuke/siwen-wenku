  The Liezi contains a parable that has become a classic illustration of how proliferation
  of choices leads to failure. When the Yang family neighbor lost a sheep, he dispatched
  both his household and requested Yang Zhu''s servants to join the search. When Yang
  Zhu inquired why so many were needed for a single sheep, the neighbor replied: "There
  are many forks in the road."


  Upon the party''s return, Yang Zhu asked if they had found the sheep. "It is lost,"
  they replied. "How did you lose it?" "The road branched again and again," they explained.
  "At each fork there were further forks, until we did not know which path to take.
  So we turned back."


  This anecdote serves as an epistemological fable about how excessive deliberation—too
  many distinctions, too many options—results in paralysis and failure. Yang Zhu''s
  countenance grew grave; he remained silent for a long time; he did not smile for
  the entire day. His disciples, puzzled by this reaction, inquired about the cause.
  Yang Zhu would not answer.


  His student Mèngsūn Yáng later consulted Xīn Dū Zǐ, who posed a hypothetical: "Once
  there were three brothers who studied the Way of benevolence and righteousness in
  Qí and Lǔ, learning from the same teacher and returning home. Their father asked:
  ''What is the Way of benevolence and righteousness?'' The eldest said: ''Benevolence
  and righteousness compel me to preserve my body and then my name.'' The second said:
  ''Benevolence and righteousness compel me to sacrifice my body for the sake of fame.''
  The youngest said: ''Benevolence and righteousness enable me to keep both body and
  name intact.'' These three teachings are mutually contradictory yet all emanate
  from the same Confucian school. Which is correct, which is mistaken?"


  Yang Zhu replied with another parable: "There was a man who lived by a river, accustomed
  to the water, adept at swimming. He could row boats and ferry people across for
  the livelihood of a hundred families. Students who came bearing grain to learn from
  him formed whole classes, yet half of them drowned in the process. They came to
  learn swimming, not to learn drowning—yet such were the consequences. What then
  is right, what is wrong?"


  The passage concludes with Xīn Dū Zǐ''s rebuke: "The Great Way is lost because of
  many forks; a scholar is destroyed because of many methods. Learning does not arise
  from different origins, does not begin differently—yet its outcomes diverge so widely.
  Only by returning to unity, by returning to the One, can one avoid loss. You have
  grown in the master''s gate, have practiced the master''s teachings—yet you do not
  understand the master''s intention. What sorrow!"


  This story parallels the [[大學八條目|great learning''s teaching]] on focus and the Confucian
  warning against [[多方喪生|multiplicity destroying life]]. It also echoes Daoist teachings
  on [[歸根復命|returning to one''s roots]] and the [[正心|rectification of the mind]] free
  from proliferating distinctions.

  『列子』「說符篇」の有名な寓話。楊朱の隣人が羊を逃がし、多くの者を遣わして搜索させた。楊朱は奇怪に思い：「羊一匹を亡くして、何故これほど多的追手の必要なのか？」隣人は答えた：「道が多くの岐路に分かれたからだ。」


  搜索に归来した一同に羊を見つけたかと問うと、「逃げました」と。「どのようにして？」「岐路の中さらに岐路があり、どこへ向かったのか分からなくなったので、引き返したのです。」


  楊朱は聞いて、顔色を暗くし、長い間黙り通し、その日一日笑いもしなかった。この寓話は過度に 많은選択肢が反而失敗につながるという認識論的寓話である。[[大學八條目]]の専一之道や、儒家の[[多方喪生｜多方にして生を喪う]]への警告と相通じる。


  心都子は後に儒者の三兄弟の問答を引いて楊朱を責めた：「大道は多歧にして羊を失い、學者は多方にして生を喪う。學は本より異なるにあらず、本より一ならぬにあらずして、末すでに若是く異なる。唯だ同に帰り一に反して、乃ち喪失することなきに至る。」これは[[歸根復命]]の思想と通じ、表面的纷雑さに惑わされず本質を見抜き、専一的心を保つ智慧を示すものである。'

《列子·說符篇》載「楊子之鄰人亡羊」之事。其鄰人喪羊，既率其黨，又請楊子之豎追之。楊子怪而問之：「亡一羊，何追者之眾？」鄰人曰：「多歧路。」既反，問：「獲羊乎？」曰：「亡之矣。」曰：「奚亡之？」曰：「歧路之中又有歧焉，吾不知所之，所以反也。」

楊子聞之，戚然變容，不言者移時，不笑者竟日。此寓言之意義深遠：選項過多、分歧過繁，反致無所適從、一事無成。學問之道亦然——[[博學詳說]]之後貴在返約，若如[[多方喪生]]者流，「大道以多歧亡羊，學者以多方喪生」，則悖乎[[格物致知]]之本旨矣。

心都子後以儒者三兄弟問仁義之事詰楊子：三人同師而學，而所言仁義之道相互乖違——一以為愛身而後名，一以為殺身以成名，一以為身名並全。楊子以濱河操舟者為喻：「本學泅，不學溺，而利害如此。」

心都子斥之曰：「大道以多歧亡羊，學者以多方喪生。學非本不同，非本不一，而末異若是。唯歸同反一，為亡得喪。」此言與[[歸根復命]]之說相通，亦示人[[正心]]之要在於凝神定志，不為歧路所眩。
