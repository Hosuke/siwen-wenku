content_legacy: '## English


  When Ye Gongzi Gao (葉公子高) asked Confucius about governance, saying "What does a
  good government administrator do?", Confucius replied: "A good government administrator
  treats those far away as if they were close, and treats the old as if it were new."


  Mozi criticized this exchange sharply. He declared that Ye Gongzi Gao had not truly
  received an answer to his question, nor had Confucius truly responded. Did Ye Gongzi
  Gao not already know that a good administrator treats the distant as close and the
  old as new? His question was "How should one go about implementing this?" Confucius,
  however, did not answer what the questioner needed to know. Instead of teaching
  the unknowing with what is known, Confucius taught only what he himself knew to
  someone who lacked that knowledge.


  This critique exemplifies Mozi''s approach to [[為政]] (governance) and his emphasis
  on practical instruction. He valued clear, actionable guidance over elegant but
  evasive rhetoric. The passage also reveals Mozi''s critical attitude toward Confucian
  method, which he viewed as obscuring rather than illuminating the path to proper
  governance.

葉公子高問政於仲尼，曰：「善為政者，若之何？」仲尼對曰：「善為政者，遠者近之，而舊者新之。」

墨子聞之，批評此答以為未得要領。墨子以為葉公子高未得其問，仲尼亦未得其所以對。蓋葉公子高豈不知善為政者之遠者近也而舊者新是哉？彼所問者，乃所以為之若之何之方法也。仲尼乃不以人之所不智告人，而以所智告之，是答非所問也。

此論可與[[治天下若治一家]]之旨相發明，皆主切實有用之政術，而非高遠虛玄之空論。
