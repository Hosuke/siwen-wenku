content_legacy: '## English


  **"Using Music to Cultivate the Heart"** is a central philosophical doctrine found
  in the *Record of Music* (樂記) chapter of the *Book of Rites* (禮記). The passage states:
  "致樂以治心，則易直子諒之心油然生矣" — "Thoroughly applying music to govern the heart, then hearts
  of ease, uprightness, benevolence, and trust will naturally spring forth."


  This doctrine articulates a sophisticated theory of moral psychology grounded in
  the transformative power of musical engagement. The text describes a progressive
  sequence of spiritual development: when the heart of ease, uprightness, benevolence,
  and trust (易直子諒之心) arises, it generates joy (樂); joy produces tranquility (安); tranquility
  enables endurance (久); endurance leads to heavenliness (天); and heavenliness approaches
  the divine (神). The ultimate effect is that "heaven does not speak yet is trustworthy;
  the divine does not rage yet commands awe" — describing a state of natural moral
  authority arising from inner cultivation rather than external coercion.


  The companion passage emphasizes the reciprocal relationship between inner states
  and outer conduct: "Thoroughly applying ritual to govern the body produces dignity
  and reverence; dignity and reverence produce gravitas and awe. If the heart experiences
  even a moment of discord or unhappiness, base and deceitful intentions will enter.
  If one''s outward appearance experiences even a moment of undignified conduct, then
  careless and disrespectful attitudes will enter."


  The doctrine thus presents music and ritual as complementary tools of self-cultivation
  — music working on the inner heart (動於內), ritual governing outward behavior (動於外).
  When both are properly applied, the result is "內和而外順" — inner harmony expressed
  through outer compliance, such that "the people, upon seeing the color of their
  ruler''s face, would not contend with him; upon observing the ruler''s demeanor,
  would not develop ease or disrespect."


  This concept profoundly influenced later Confucian thought, particularly the *Doctrine
  of the Mean*''s discussion of "誠" (sincerity) and the Mencian theory of the [[四端]]
  (four beginnings). It establishes music not merely as aesthetic entertainment but
  as a primary technology of moral self-cultivation, capable of transforming the deepest
  structures of the human heart. The [[誠-儒學]] (concept of sincerity) and 致樂以治心 are
  thus complementary doctrines — music produces the emotional conditions (易直子諒) in
  which sincerity can flourish.

**「致樂以治心」**為《禮記·樂記》所載之重要儒學命題。其文云：「致樂以治心，則易直子諒之心油然生矣。易直子諒之心生則樂，樂則安，安則久，久則天，天則神。天則不言而信，神則不怒而威。」

此命題闡發音樂修養心性之理，具有深邃之心性論意涵。「易直子諒之心」即平和、正直、仁愛、誠信之心，乃道德修養之根本情緒基礎。樂之功效在於由此根本情緒之生發，而漸次臻於「不言而信、不怒而威」之聖王境界。

《樂記》又論禮之功用：「致禮以治躬則莊敬，莊敬則嚴威。心中斯須不和不樂，而鄙詐之心入之矣。外貌斯須不莊不敬，而易慢之心入之矣。」此論禮樂內外交養之道：樂主動於內，禮主動於外，二者相須為用，方能成就完人之德行。

樂之於心，如春風之於萬物，「其感人深，其移風易俗」（《樂記》），故先王「著其教焉」，以之為教化百姓之重要途徑。此說與《論語》「興於詩，立於禮，成於樂」之教相互發明，確立音樂在儒學修身功夫中之根本地位。

「致樂以治心」與[[誠-儒學]]關係密切：易直子諒之心即誠之情感基礎，而樂之功效在於使人返其性情之正、去其偽飾，复歸於真。[[修身]]之道，捨此何以臻之？
