content_legacy: '## English


  Mozi observes a fundamental contradiction in worldly gentlemen: when they are poor,
  they resent being called poor and become indignant; yet when they are without righteousness,
  they are pleased to be called righteous. Mozi declares this fundamentally absurd.


  This passage reveals Mozi''s penetrating critique of moral hypocrisy. The [[義-儒學-之|worldly
  concern with reputation]] (名) over substance troubles Mozi greatly. A true gentleman
  should pursue [[明善誠身]] (clarifying goodness and sincerity in self-cultivation),
  not take pleasure in empty labels. The passage supports Mozi''s broader argument
  that [[義]] must be pursued for its actual benefit to the world, not merely for the
  reputation it brings.


  This critique connects to Mozi''s teaching that [[言足以復行者]] (words sufficient to
  be acted upon) should guide discourse, while empty claims to righteousness that
  cannot be implemented are merely [[蕩口]] (idle chatter).

墨子論世俗君子之乖謬：彼輩貧而謂之富則怒，無義而謂之有義則喜。墨子以為此豈不悖哉！

此論揭示墨子對道德偽善之深刻批判。世人之[[義-儒學-之|汲汲於名]]者，令墨子深感憂慮。真正的君子當以求[[明善誠身]]為務，而非喜悅於空洞之名號。此與上文[[天下之良寶]]之義相表裏，皆明真正的義行當以利民為準，非以博取虛名為歸。
