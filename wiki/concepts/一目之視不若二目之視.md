content_legacy: '## English


  "One eye''s vision is not as good as two eyes'', one ear''s hearing is not as good
  as two ears'', one hand''s grasp is not as good as two hands'' strength" is a famous
  analogy from "Mozi: Shang Tong Xia." Mozi used this to illustrate why rulers must
  seek counsel from many sources.


  Mozi argued that the sage-kings of antiquity achieved their governance not through
  personal omniscience but through trusting others to act on their behalf. They could
  hear about events across the realm and respond to affairs before they occurred,
  all because they relied on the collective efforts of many capable ministers and
  advisors. Even with the keenest eyes and ears, a ruler cannot personally see everything
  a thousand li away or personally hear everything in all directions. However, by
  appointing virtuous ministers and establishing efficient communication systems,
  the sage-king could learn of merit and demerit anywhere in the world.


  The implication is profound: good governance requires not merely a wise ruler but
  also a responsive information network that allows the ruler to know the true state
  of affairs below. Without such a network, even the most well-intentioned ruler would
  be unable to reward the good or punish the wicked.

「一目之視也，不若二目之視也；一耳之聽也，不若二耳之聽也；一手之操也，不若二手之彊也」語出《墨子·尚同下》，乃墨子以感官之喻說明廣納諫諍之重要。

墨子之意，以為聖王之治天下，非一人之智力所能獨任也。千里之外有賢人焉，聖王得而賞之；千里之外有暴人焉，聖王得而罰之。然聖王非能往視而就聽也，何由知之？曰：唯能信身而從事，故利若此。聖王委任賢能，上下相通，故能不出戶庭而周知四方。

此說與《尚同》之義相表裡。尚同者，非以上制下、以威臨民也，乃以一同天下之義，上下相通，視聽相及，使天下如一人之視聽然。苟能若是，則姦巧盜賊無所遁形，而天下治矣。

故墨子以為，人之視聽有限，而天下之事無窮，唯賴衆人之耳目以為耳目，則雖聖人亦可以治天下也。此亦《老子》「聖人無常心，以百姓心為心」之意，而墨子言之尤詳焉。
