  **Marriage Regulations** (嫁娶之法) appear in *On Frugality* as an integral component
  of sage governance. Mozi cites the laws of the sage kings requiring men aged twenty
  to marry and establish households, and women aged fifteen to serve their husbands.
  These regulations aimed at populating the realm.


  The text explains that human population is inherently difficult to multiply. While
  some suggest the number of luxury goods can be doubled through frugality, the real
  challenge is human reproduction. The sage kings understood this and established
  marriage laws to ensure early and timely unions.


  When the sage kings perished, the regulations fell into disuse. People married early
  or late according to their circumstances, resulting in a net decrease in population
  over generations. Mozi calculates that if the sage king''s ten-year standard were
  maintained, three years of childbearing could produce offspring. By marrying early,
  the population could indeed double.

**嫁娶之法**者，《節用上》所言聖王之制也。墨子引聖王之法曰：「丈夫年二十，毋敢不處家。女子年十五，毋敢不事人。」此聖王所以繁人族之法也。

墨子論「人為難倍」之理：物之數可加倍，而人則難以倍增。聖王既沒，此法不行，民有早婚者二十年處家，有晚婚者四十年處家，以遲早相踐，距聖王之法十年。若純用三年而字，子生可以二三年矣，是故蚤處家可以倍也。

此法與[[節用]]相輔：去無用之費，以足生養之資；定嫁娶之時，以繁天下之民。二者並行，則國富人多，此聖王之治道也。
