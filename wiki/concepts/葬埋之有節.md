  This concept presents Mozi''s argument that burial practices must observe proper
  limits, drawing an analogy with the consumption of food and clothing. Mozi observes
  that while clothing and food are matters of life-benefit (生利) for the living and
  should be used with moderation, burial practices are matters of death-benefit (死利)
  for the deceased and should likewise observe节制.


  The key passage states: "Clothing and food are the means by which people generate
  benefit in life, and yet there is proper moderation in these. How much more so should
  there be moderation in burial, which is the means by which people generate benefit
  in death?" This represents Mozi''s logical argument that if even the necessary consumption
  of the living requires moderation, then the expenditure on funerals—which serves
  only the dead—must certainly require restraint.


  Mozi''s argument establishes a principle of proportionality in funeral expenditures.
  He contends that excessive burial spending harms the living without benefiting the
  dead, violating basic economic and moral principles. The concept connects to his
  broader teachings on [[節用]] (frugality) and his critique of rituals that waste resources
  while failing to produce genuine social benefit.

葬埋之有節，乃墨子論喪葬當有節制之法也。墨子以為，人生在世，衣食者，人之生利也，然且猶尚有節；葬埋者，人之死利也，夫何獨無節於此乎？

夫人之所以生者，衣食是賴，然聖王制為節用之教，使天下之人大取於民而不匱。夫葬埋之事，專為死者之利，而死者無知，厚斂之財悉歸糞土，豈非無益之甚者乎？

墨子之意，以為生者之利尚當節用，況死者之無益乎？棺三寸足以朽體，衣三領足以覆惡，壟若參耕之畝，則止矣。此聖王之節也。厚葬久喪者不然，竭家室之財以事死者，而生人不得衣食，其害不可勝言。

是以墨子之論葬埋，必以有節為尚，以為厚葬之非。此與[[節用]]之教相通，皆墨子興利除害之旨也。
