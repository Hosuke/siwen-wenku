content_legacy: '## English


  **容儀** (Róng yí, "ceremonial demeanor") refers to the Confucian system of prescribed
  physical bearing, expression, and conduct appropriate to different ritual and social
  contexts. The *Book of Rites* chapter "Jade Ornaments" provides one of the most
  systematic treatments of this topic in the classical corpus, specifying precise
  rules for posture, gaze, movement, and expression in daily life, at court, and during
  mourning.


  The text enumerates the essential qualities of proper demeanor:


  "**足容重**，**手容恭**，**目容端**，**口容止**，**聲容靜**，**頭容直**，**氣容肅**，**立容德**，**色容莊**，**坐如尸**，**燕居告溫溫**"


  - Foot bearing: heavy and stable

  - Hand bearing: respectful

  - Eye bearing: upright

  - Mouth bearing: controlled

  - Voice: quiet

  - Head: straight

  - Breath: solemn

  - Standing: virtuous bearing

  - Expression: grave

  - Sitting: like a ritual corpse (i.e., still and centered)

  - In informal residence: warm and gentle in speech


  This systematic enumeration reveals the Confucian understanding that **the body
  is a vehicle for moral expression**. Physical postures are not merely aesthetic
  but carry ethical weight. The phrase "坐如尸" (sitting like a sacrificial victim/ancestor)
  is particularly significant — it demands that even in private, one maintain the
  centered stillness appropriate to ritual contexts.


  The text then distinguishes ceremonial and mourning contexts: "凡祭，容貌顏色，如見所祭者。喪容纍纍，色容顛顛，視容瞿瞿梅梅，言容繭繭。戎容暨暨，言容詻詻，色容厲肅，視容清明。"


  For sacrifice, one''s appearance must be as if actually seeing the recipient of
  the offering. For mourning, the bearing should be weary (纍纍), the complexion haggard
  (顛顛), the gaze startled (瞿瞿梅梅), and the speech weak (繭繭). For military contexts,
  the bearing should be resolute (暨暨), the speech sharp (詻詻), the complexion stern
  (厲肅), and the gaze clear (清明).


  The chapter also describes walking: "端行，頤溜如矢，弁行，剡剡起屨，執龜玉，舉前曳踵，蹜蹜如也。" Walking with
  dignity, the chin flowing like an arrow; walking quickly with the ceremonial cap,
  lifting the feet high; carrying ritual objects, sliding forward with careful steps.
  Each form of movement corresponds to a specific context and character.


  "**立容辨，卑毋諂，頭頸必中，山立時行，盛氣顛實，揚休玉色**" — Standing bearing should discriminate (i.e.,
  be appropriate to context), be humble without being sycophantic, the head and neck
  centered, like a mountain standing and moving at the proper time, with breath full
  and complexion shining like jade.'

**容儀**者，人之容貌威儀所以接物待人者也。《禮記·玉藻》詳載其制，為後世禮書之楷模。

首節言君子日常之容：「足容重，手容恭，目容端，口容止，聲容靜，頭容直，氣容肅，立容德，色容莊，坐如尸，燕居告溫溫。」
