content_legacy: '## English


  The **"Minor Rules of Deportment"** (少儀) records a specific ritual formula for conducting
  divination (卜筮): when seeking guidance through oracular means, one asks two questions:


  > **「義與？志與？」**

  > "Is it righteous? Is it from intention?"


  This formula represents a sophisticated epistemological framework for evaluating
  divination responses. The two questions address distinct but complementary aspects
  of proposed action:


  **義 (Righteousness)** concerns whether the action itself is objectively appropriate
  according to moral principle. A divination may confirm favorable circumstances,
  but if the proposed action violates [[義-儒學-之|righteousness]] (such as harming others
  unjustly), the reading should be disregarded.


  **志 (Intention)** concerns whether one''s own motivations are pure. Even an objectively
  righteous action may be undertaken for wrong reasons (self-interest disguised as
  virtue), and such intentions undermine the moral worth of the deed.


  The text then specifies:


  > **義則可問，志則否。**

  > "If [the response concerns] righteousness, one may inquire; if [it concerns] one''s
  intention, one may not."


  This means: if the divination asks whether your action aligns with righteousness,
  the oracle is competent to answer. But if it asks whether your personal intentions
  are pure, the divination cannot reliably judge—self-knowledge must come from other
  sources.


  This principle reflects a broader Confucian concern with the relationship between
  external outcomes and internal moral cultivation. The *Mencius* discusses how [[義-儒學-之|righteousness]]
  must be grounded in genuine moral feeling rather than calculated advantage. The
  *Doctrine of the Mean* emphasizes that true sincerity (誠) arises from self-reflection
  rather than external verification.


  The formula also implicitly assumes that **不貳問** (one should not ask twice) is a
  principle of ritual propriety—doubting the oracle after receiving an answer demonstrates
  lack of faith rather than appropriate caution.

《禮記·少儀》記卜筮之辭曰：

> **「義與？志與？」**

此為卜筮時請命之辭，鄭玄注云：「問事之宜與不也。」孔穎達疏云：「義，正事也；志，私意也。」

所言者何？卜筮所以決疑，然卜筮非萬能，有可問者，有不可問者。

**「義與」**者，問此事之當為與否，即其事理是否合乎正道也。若所行之事前合乎義，則可決之；若不合義，縱得他吉之兆，亦不可從。故卜筮之要，在於先判斷事之是非。

**「志與」**者，問我心之正否，即己意是否無私也。然心術之正與否，難以卜筮決之。人心幽微，唯己自知，他人莫能測，況龜蓍乎？

經文明言：

> **義則可問，志則否。**

言若卜筮問事之宜義與否，則可決也；若欲問己心之志，則不可問也。此與《論語》「內省不疚」之意相通。[[仁-儒學-之|仁者]] 自反諸己，[[知言]] 所以辨人心之邪正。

又言 **「不貳問」**，蓋一事一卜而已，不可反覆占問以求私意。《易》曰：「初筮告，再三瀆，瀆則不告。」正此之謂。

此問辭之設，見儒家於卜筮之態度：卜筮所以輔助決疑，非所以測度天意；問義不問志，乃見儒者自反之學術精神也。
