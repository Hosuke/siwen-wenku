content_legacy: '## English


  **Eliminating the Six Biases** (必去六辟) is Mozi''s teaching on psychological self-cultivation,
  found in the *Guiyi* chapter. The "six biases" (*六辟*) are six emotional states that
  obstruct moral judgment: joy (*喜*), anger (*怒*), pleasure (*樂*), sorrow (*悲*), love
  (*愛*), and hatred (*惡*). Mozi teaches that one must eliminate all six and practice
  *仁義* (benevolence-righteousness) instead.


  The parallel passage states: "When the mouth, nose, hands, feet, and ears all engage
  in righteousness (*從事於義*), one will surely become a sage." This suggests that moral
  transformation requires not merely intellectual understanding but embodied practice—righteousness
  must be enacted through all bodily faculties, not just the mind.


  This doctrine contrasts sharply with Confucian approaches to emotion. Where Confucianism
  seeks to regulate and transform emotions through ritual (*禮*), Mozi advocates their
  complete removal. This may reflect the Mohist emphasis on *utility* (用): emotional
  attachments create partiality (*別*) and obstruct the impartial care (*兼愛*) that
  benefits all.


  The passage concludes with an analogy: just as a craftsman who cannot cut straight
  does not reject the carpenter''s line but blames his own skill, so too should practitioners
  of righteousness blame themselves when they fail, not reject the Way of righteousness.


  See also: [[修身]], [[兼別之辨]], [[仁-儒學-之]]

**必去六辟**出於《墨子·貴義》，為墨子修身之要道。六辟者，六種情感之偏蔽也：喜、怒、樂、悲、愛、惡。墨子以為，此六者不去，則義不可行，必待六者盡除，而後仁義可施也。

夫喜、怒、樂、悲、愛、惡，皆人之常情，而墨子以為偏蔽者，何也？蓋仁者愛人，不當有偏；義者宜事，不當有私。六辟者，偏之所在也。喜則私其所喜，怒則害其所怒，愛則害其所不爱，是皆與[[兼愛]]之道相悖也。墨子之義，在兼而愛之，不別親疏，若懷喜愛之偏，則不能兼矣。

墨子又云：「手足口鼻耳從事於義，必為聖人。」此言聖人之境，非徒心知之，更要身行之。四肢五官，皆從事於義，則表裏如一、知行合一，而聖人可致矣。

故墨子以匠者為喻：匠人斵木而不能成直，豈怨繩墨之不正哉？當自責其藝之拙耳。行道而不能成義，亦若是也。是故為義而不能，必無排其道；道無可排，當反求諸己。
