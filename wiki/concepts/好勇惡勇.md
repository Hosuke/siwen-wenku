content_legacy: '## English


  When Mozi told Luo Hua-li (駱滑氂) that he had heard Luo delighted in courage (好勇),
  Luo replied: "Indeed, whenever I hear there is a brave man in my village, I must
  follow and kill him."


  Mozi declared: "All under heaven desire what they love and dislike what they hate.
  But you, upon hearing there is a brave man in your village, must follow and kill
  him. This is not delighting in courage—this is hating courage!"


  This dialogue exposes a crucial distinction. True courage (大勇) cannot be demonstrated
  by killing others. Rather, as Mozi elsewhere teaches, true valor lies in [[行一不義殺一不辜]]
  (performing one unjust act or killing one innocent person is already too much).
  The external show of aggression masked a deeper hatred of genuine valor. This relates
  to Mozi''s broader ethical framework where [[義與信和與仁]] (righteousness, trust, harmony,
  and benevolence) guide proper conduct, and to his critique that actions driven by
  hatred cannot constitute true virtue.

墨子謂駱滑氂曰：「吾聞子好勇。」駱滑氂曰：「然，我聞其鄉有勇士焉，吾必從而殺之。」墨子曰：「天下莫不欲與其所好，度其所惡，今子聞其鄉有勇士焉，必從而殺之，是非好勇也，是惡勇也。」

此對話揭示一重要分辨。真正的勇非以殺人表現。墨子他處所教，真正之勇在於[[行一不義殺一不辜]]，不行不義、不殺無辜。外顯之攻擊性，實掩內在對真正勇氣之厭惡。此可與[[大勇]]之說相發明。
