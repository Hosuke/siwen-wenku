content_legacy: '## English


  The "合巹而酳" (hé jǐn ér yìn) ceremony is a ritual performed during the Confucian wedding
  (昏禮), where the bride and groom drink together from a cup made by splitting a gourd
  (瓠). This practice symbolizes the union of the couple into one entity, marking the
  formal establishment of their marital bond. The ceremony represents the culmination
  of the wedding ritual, signifying that the two individuals, previously separate,
  are now joined as one. According to the *Book of Rites* (《禮記》), the shared drinking
  of the cup serves to "合體同尊卑以親之" — merging their bodies and equalizing their status
  to foster mutual affection. The gourd, which grows as a single fruit but splits
  upon ripening, metaphorically represents the complementary nature of husband and
  wife, two halves that together form a complete whole. This ritual not only formalized
  the marriage but also embodied the Confucian ideals of harmony, unity, and mutual
  respect within the household.

合巹而酳，乃昏禮之終儀。昏禮既成，新人共飲一器。所用之器，乃瓠瓜剖而為之者也。《禮記·昏義》云：「共牢而食，合巹而酳；所以合體同尊卑以親之也。」言其所以合二姓之好者，必於此時成之。瓠瓜同蒂而生，至熟則剖，是故以喻夫婦之義——陰陽相配，判而為二，合乃成一也。共牢者，同食一牲；合巹者，同飲一器。食以成恩，酳以定分。恩既成，分既定，則夫婦之道立矣。是禮也，非特為燕飲之私，乃所以正夫婦之始，使知合體同尊卑之義也。
