## English

The passage on "Offerings at the Moment of Death" (始死之奠) in *Tan Gong I* addresses the earliest stage of mourning rituals and the proper disposition of remaining food provisions. When someone dies, the living must immediately consider how to honor the deceased through proper offerings.

The text establishes a graduated sequence of mourning obligations:

1. **Three days to encoffining**: "喪三日而殯，凡附於身者，必誠必信，勿之有悔焉耳矣。" — All items placed with the body must be genuine and sincere, with no room for regret.

2. **Three months to burial**: "三月而葬，凡附於棺者，必誠必信，勿之有悔焉耳矣。" — Everything accompanying the coffin must be genuine and sincere, with no room for regret.

3. **Three years as the extreme limit**: "喪三年以為極，亡則弗之忘矣。" — Three years is the limit of mourning; even after the deceased is gone, they should not be forgotten.

4. **Yearly remembrance**: "故君子有終身之憂，而無一朝之患。故忌日不樂。" — The superior person carries lifelong concern and avoids any sudden misfortune. On anniversary days of death, one does not celebrate.
