## English

Filial Piety, Brotherhood, Loyalty, and Obedience (孝弟忠順) constitute the Fourfold Virtues that define the moral obligations of an adult person in classical Confucianism, as articulated in the *Book of Rites* chapter "Meaning of the Capping Ceremony" (冠義). These four virtues represent the fundamental behavioral expectations that emerge from the proper performance of the capping ceremony (冠禮).

The concept arises from the text's discussion of what becomes possible after a person has been properly capped and given their adult style name (字). The text states that the newly adult person must now fulfill the rituals of being a son, a younger brother, a minister, and a junior person in society. Specifically, this means:

- **Filial Piety (孝)**: The virtue of honoring and serving one's parents with proper reverence, providing for them in life, and mourning them appropriately in death. This is the vertical bond between parent and child.

- **Brotherhood (弟)**: The virtue of respectful interaction between siblings, particularly the deference of younger brothers to elder brothers. This extends to all hierarchical relationships within the family and society.

- **Loyalty (忠)**: The virtue of sincerity and dedication in one's service to superiors, particularly in the political sphere as a minister serving the ruler. This is the vertical bond between ruler and minister.

- **Obedience (順)**: The virtue of harmonious compliance with proper social roles and expectations, particularly as a younger person in relation to elders. This is the horizontal dimension of social harmony.

The *Book of Rites* presents these four virtues as interdependent and mutually reinforcing. Only when "filial piety and brotherhood, loyalty and obedience are established" can a person truly "become a human being" and be qualified to "govern others." The text explicitly links this to the concept of [[修身]] (self-cultivation), stating that the establishment of these four virtues is the prerequisite for both personal moral development and the capacity for political leadership.

The concept connects to the broader Confucian doctrine of the Five Relationships (五倫) and the virtue of [[仁-儒學-之|ren]] (benevolence), as these four virtues represent the specific behavioral expressions of ren in the four primary social relationships: parent-child, sibling, ruler-minister, and elder-younger.

## 中文

孝弟忠順，乃儒家論述成人之後所當踐行之四德，見於《禮記·冠義》篇。其義以為，人既行冠禮、得字之後，當盡孝弟忠順之行者，乃可以為人、可以治人。

孝者，善事父母之道也。《論語》有云：「弟子入則孝，出則弟」，孝為人倫之大本。蓋父母之恩，昊天罔極，為人子者，當竭力奉養，以禮致敬。此上下之義也。

弟者，善事兄長之道也，亦通於泛言之敬長。《冠義》云「見於兄弟，兄弟拜之」，明兄弟之倫，弟當敬兄，兄當愛弟，相親相敬，以和家庭。此長幼之序也。

忠者，臣事君之道也。《冠義》所言「為人臣」之禮，強調在下位者當盡心竭力，輔佐君上，以忠貞為本。此君臣之義也。

順者，少事長之道也。《冠義》所言「為人少者之禮行焉」，謂年幼之人當敬順年長者，不敢以少陵長，此亦長幼之序推而廣之者也。

四者之義，非各自獨立，乃相需為用者也。《冠義》云：「孝弟忠順之行立，而後可以為人；可以為人，而後可以治人也。」是故四德立，則修身之功成；修身成，則齊家治國平天下之道備矣。四者之中，孝弟又為根本，忠順由之而推。故曾子曰：「慎終追遠，民德歸厚矣」，此之謂也。

## 日本語

孝弟忠順（こうていちゅうじゅん）とは、儒家の礼学において、成人の際に修めすべき四つの徳目を指す。『礼記』「冠義」篇に论述されているように、冠礼（guanli）を完了し字（じ）を授かった者は、孝・弟・忠・順の四徳を実行する義務を負う。

**孝（こう）**：父母を敬い仕える徳。子は родительの恩に報いるため、生きる間は奉養し、死後は適切に喪に服する。これは垂直的な親子の紐帯を表す。

**弟（てい）**：兄弟間、特に年下の弟が兄を敬う徳。家族内の階層的関係から社会全般へと拡張される。

**忠（ちゅう）**：上位者、特に臣下が君主に仕える際の誠実さと献身の徳。政治的領域における君臣の垂直的紐帯を表す。

**順（じゅん）**：適切な社会的役割と期待への調和的な従順、特に年少者が年長者を扱う際の徳。社会和合の水平的次元を表す。

この四徳は相互に依存し、相互に強化し合う。『礼記』は明確に述べる：「孝弟忠順之行立せて、而る後に人以って為ることを可し；人以って為ることを可して、而る後に人以って治むことを可す」と。[[修身]]の実践はこれらの徳の確立を前提とし、同時にそれを確実にする。
