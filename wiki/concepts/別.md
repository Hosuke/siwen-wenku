content_legacy: '## English


  In Mozi''s philosophy, "Bie" (別, also written as 別) represents partiality, discrimination,
  and self-interested action — the antithesis of [[兼愛]] (universal love). While *jian
  ai* means to love all equally without distinction, *bie* means to love some while
  hating others, to treat one''s own people well while harming foreigners.


  The text states explicitly: "Those who follow Heaven''s will — this is called ''jian''
  (兼, union/incorporation); those who oppose Heaven''s will — this is called ''bie''
  (別, separation/division)." The path of *jian* uses righteousness (義) to correct,
  while the path of *bie* uses force (力) to correct.


  Those on the path of *bie* do the opposite of *jian*: the large attack the small,
  the strong humiliate the weak, the many harm the few, the cunning deceive the foolish,
  the noble despise the humble, the wealthy mock the poor, the robust rob the elderly.
  States governed by *bie* use fire, water, poison, and weapons to destroy each other.
  Their governance benefits neither Heaven above, nor the spirits in the middle, nor
  the people below — threefold non-benefit with nothing beneficial. Such rulers are
  labeled "bandits, thieves, the unrighteous, the disloyal, the unkind, the unfilial,"
  and evil names gather about them from across the world.


  The ancient sage-kings (堯舜禹湯文武) practiced *jian*, loving all under heaven equally,
  and were rewarded by Heaven with dominion. The wicked kings (桀紂幽厲) practiced *bie*,
  hating all under heaven, and were punished by Heaven with destruction. Thus, *bie*
  is the path of [[天賊]] (Heavenly Outcast), opposed to [[天德]] (Heavenly Virtue), and
  fundamentally contrary to [[順天之意]] (following Heaven''s will).

別，乃[[墨學]]重要思想，與兼相對。兼者，兼愛天下，無所分別；別者，愛己而惡人，厚此而薄彼。

《天志下》明言：「順天之意者，兼也；[[反天之意]]者，別也。兼之為道也義正，別之為道也力正。」別者以力正人，非以義正人；兼者以義正人，非以力正人。

行別之道者，大則攻小、强則侮弱、衆則賊寡、詐則欺愚、貴則傲賤、富則驕貧、壯則奪老。天下庶國方以水火毒藥兵刃相賊害。事上不利天、中不利鬼、下不利人，三不利而無所利。此之謂[[天賊]]。

古聖王行兼，故天賞之；暴王行別，故天罰之。別與兼相反，天德與天賊亦由此分。
