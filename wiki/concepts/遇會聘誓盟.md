  ## English


  The "Qu Li Xia" chapter of the *Book of Rites* establishes a comprehensive framework
  for inter-state diplomatic engagements, distinguishing five distinct categories
  based on timing, formality, and purpose.


  The text states: "諸侯未及期相見曰『遇』，相見於郤地曰『會』；諸侯使大夫問於諸侯曰『聘』，約信曰『誓』，蒞牲曰『盟』."


  These five categories represent different levels of diplomatic engagement:


  1. **遇 (Yù)** — Ad hoc meetings that occur before the scheduled time. When two lords
  meet unexpectedly or before the appointed date, this informal encounter is called
  *yù* (to meet by chance). It requires no elaborate preparation but establishes initial
  contact.


  2. **會 (Huì)** — Formal assemblies held at a neutral location (*郤地*, literally "a
  gap place" or border territory). These are pre-arranged meetings between lords,
  often involving multiple participants, serving as forums for negotiation, alliance-building,
  or ritual displays of power. The Spring and Autumn period saw numerous such assemblies.


  3. **聘 (Pìn)** — Diplomatic missions where a lord dispatches a high official (usually
  a minister or大夫) to inquire after another lord''s wellbeing. This demonstrates respect
  and maintains friendly relations. The dispatched official brings gifts and carries
  formal messages.


  4. **誓 (Shì)** — Oaths taken to establish mutual commitments. When lords make agreements
  and bind themselves through verbal promises (*約信*), they take oaths to solemnize
  their compact.'
