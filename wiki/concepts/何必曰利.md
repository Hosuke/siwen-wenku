content_legacy: '## English


  **"Why Speak of Profit?"** (*何必曰利*) is a foundational concept in Mencius'' political
  philosophy, derived from his first dialogue with King Hui of Liang. When the king
  asked what benefit Mencius could bring to his state, Mencius responded by rejecting
  the premise of the question itself, declaring: "Why must Your Majesty speak of profit?
  There is only Righteousness and Benevolence."


  Mencius argued that if a ruler prioritizes profit, then ministers, officials, and
  commoners will each seek personal gain, creating a situation where "all levels compete
  for profit, and the state is endangered." He illustrated this with the famous warning
  that in a state of ten thousand chariots, one who assassinates the ruler will come
  from a family of a thousand chariots; in a state of a thousand chariots, the assassin
  will come from a family of a hundred chariots. Those who put profit before righteousness
  "will not be satisfied until they have seized everything."


  The corollary, Mencius insisted, is that no benevolent person has ever abandoned
  their parents, and no righteous person has ever neglected their ruler. Therefore,
  the Way of a true king is simply to practice [[仁-儒學-之]] and [[義-儒學-之]], not to pursue
  profit.


  This argument established a key principle in Confucian political thought: governance
  must be grounded in moral virtue rather than material calculation. The concept directly
  opposes Legalist doctrines that emphasized rewards and punishments as the primary
  tools of statecraft.

**何必曰利**，語出《孟子·梁惠王上》，為孟子見梁惠王時所言。惠王問何以利吾國，孟子對曰：「王何必曰利？亦有仁義而已矣。」繼而申論：「王曰『何以利吾國』，大夫曰『何以利吾家』，士庶人曰『何以利吾身』，上下交征利而國危矣。」

孟子此論，以利與仁義為對待。天下之人皆言利，則人人私其所獲，上悖君，下殃民。萬乘之國，弒君者必千乘之家；千乘之國，弒君者必百乘之家。苟為後義而先利，不奪不饜。

然則未有仁而遺其親者，未有義而後其君者。是故為國者，但行仁義，自可王天下，何必言利？此說直切戰國縱橫之風，力闢功利之學，為後世儒者定論。

 [[義-儒學-之]] 先於 [[利-儒學-之]]，此儒家政治哲學之要旨也。
