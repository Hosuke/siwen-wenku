content_legacy: '## English


  The practice of "rewarding at the ancestral temple and punishing at the she altar"
  (賞於祖僇於社) represents a central ritual mechanism for enforcing moral order in ancient
  Chinese governance, discussed extensively in Mozi''s "Exposing the Ghosts" (明鬼下).
  This principle holds that rulers should conduct commendations at the ancestral temple
  and executions at the communal altar, thereby invoking supernatural authority to
  legitimize their decisions.


  Mozi cites the Xia dynasty''s "Yu''s Oath" (禹誓) as evidence that this practice dates
  to earliest antiquity. When Yu marched against Youyi, he declared: "Rewards shall
  be at the ancestral temple, and punishments at the altar." The explanation given
  was that rewards at the ancestral temple represented the equal distribution of mandates,
  while punishments at the altar represented correct adjudication of legal cases.
  By linking worldly justice to sacred space, the ancient kings established a system
  where moral decisions carried cosmic weight.


  The Duke of Zhou similarly instituted that sage-kings would distribute sacrifices
  according to rank after the Zhou conquest of Shang: "relatives received internal
  sacrifices, and outsiders received external sacrifices." This allocation of sacrificial
  privileges reinforced hierarchical order while demonstrating that the new dynasty
  had inherited Heaven''s mandate, which was legitimized through proper ritual performance.


  Mozi uses this principle to argue that proper ritual—particularly honoring ancestors
  and communal altars—is essential to good governance. When officials witness the
  ruler''s commitment to ritual propriety and see that commendations occur at sacred
  sites, they are motivated to maintain clean administration. Criminals who witness
  supernatural enforcement of justice are deterred from wrongdoing. The integration
  of ritual space and political authority thus serves as a foundation for social order.


  See also: [[祭百神]], [[社稷]], [[鬼神之賞賢而罰暴]], [[三代治道]]

賞於祖僇於社，乃古聖王賞善罰惡之制，見於《墨子·明鬼下》。墨子引《夏書·禹誓》以證其古，謂禹征有扈，誓曰「賞於祖，僇於社」，蓋賞功罰罪，必於祖廟社稷行之，以明賞罰之公，兼資鬼神以為監察。

祖者，祖廟也。賞於祖者，何哉？墨子釋之曰「言分命之均」，言分授之命，賞之以均，不敢獨私也。社者，社稷也。僇於社者，何哉？墨子釋之曰「言聽獄之事」，言聽斷之公，罰之以正，不敢枉法也。夫賞罰於祖廟社稷，是將賞罰之事，付之祖宗神明，使受賞者感祖宗之恩，受罰者畏神明之誅，此古聖王神道設教之意也。

周武王之伐殷誅紂也，亦使諸侯分其祭祀，曰「使親者受內祀，疏者受外祀」。武王必以鬼神為有，故能行之。若鬼神無有，則武王何必祭分之哉？此墨子所以據以為鬼神實有之證也。

墨子又言：「古聖王必以鬼神為賞賢而罰暴，是故賞必於祖，而僇必於社。」賞賢罰暴，配之祖社，此古聖王之通義，而為政之要道也。

所出：《墨子·明鬼下》
