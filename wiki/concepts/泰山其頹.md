content_legacy: '## English


  The "Mountain Collapse Song" (泰山其頹) represents one of the most poignant moments
  in the *Book of Rites* (*Liji*), capturing Confucius''s final meditation on his
  own mortality and legacy. According to the text, Confucius rose early, walked with
  hands clasped behind his back, leisurely at his gate, and sang:


  「泰山其頹乎，梁木其壞乎，哲人其萎乎？」


  "Alas! The great mountain collapses! The chief beam breaks! The wise man withers!"


  After finishing the song, he entered and sat by the door. When Zigong (子貢) heard
  this, he recognized the ominous nature of the words. He reasoned: "If the great
  mountain collapses, what shall I look up to? If the chief beam breaks and the wise
  man withers, what shall I depend upon? The Master is surely about to fall ill."


  Zigong rushed to enter, and Confucius told him the timing of his death was approaching,
  citing that he dreamed of sitting between the two pillars of the hall—indicating
  he was to receive the honors of a ruler. He explained that the three dynasties had
  different mourning customs: the Xia placed the coffin on the eastern steps, the
  Yin between the two pillars facing guests and host, and the Zhou on the western
  steps treating the deceased as a guest. As a man of Yin, Confucius''s dream of sitting
  between the pillars signified his imminent death.


  This passage reveals several key aspects of Confucian thought: the interconnection
  between cosmic signs and human affairs, the sage''s awareness of his own destiny,
  and the importance placed on proper ritual positioning even in death. The imagery
  of mountain collapse and beam-breaking symbolized the loss of social and moral order.


  After approximately seven days of illness, Confucius passed away. Following his
  death, his disciples debated what mourning garments they should wear, uncertain
  how to honor their teacher. Zixia (子夏) proposed: "Formerly the Master, in mourning
  for Yanyuan [顔淵], appeared as if he had lost a son without adopting mourning garments.
  The same was true when mourning for Zilu [子路]. I propose we mourn for the Master
  as for a father without adopting mourning garments."



  ## 日本語


  「泰山其頹」は『礼記・檀弓上』に見える孔子の最期の歌である。全文は次の通り：


  「泰山其頹乎、梁木其壞乎、哲人其萎乎」


  （泰山は崩れんか、梁木は朽ちんか、哲人は枯れんか）


  。『礼記』によれば、孔子は早朝に起き、両手を背后で組み、杖を曳いて門前を逍遥し、この歌を歌った。そして歌畢って入り、戸口に座した。子貢がこれを聞き駆け付けると、「泰山崩れれば我将に何を仰がんや、梁木朽ちれば哲人枯れれば我将に何を置かんや。夫子はまさに病を得たまへり」と悟った。


  孔子は己の夢について語り自分は殷人の末裔であり、両楹の間に奠せられたことは死の兆しだと告げた。夏は東階に殯し、殷は両楹の間に殯し、周は西階に殯して客待遇した。自分は殷人であるから、夢の両楹の間は死の予兆だと。七日の後、孔子は逝った。


  門人たちは喪服について議論し、子貢は顔淵や子路を喪った時の例を引き、「父を喪うが如く無服にて喪うべし」と提案した。公西赤は周的覆幕に殷の蟻結、夏の旐を立て、孔子の葬りに三代折衷の礼を尽くした。'

「泰山其頹」見於《禮記·檀弓上》，為孔子臨終之歌，辭曰：

「泰山其頹乎，梁木其壞乎，哲人其萎乎？」

據載，孔子蚤作，負手曳杖，消搖於門而歌。既歌而入，當戶而坐。子貢聞之，趨而入見，謂「泰山其頹，則吾將安仰？梁木其壞、哲人其萎，則吾將安放？夫子殆將病也。」

孔子告以夢坐奠於兩楹之間，自言為殷人後裔，當受王者之奠。乃言夏后氏殯於東階，殷人殯於兩楹之間，周人殯於西階而猶賓之。孔子夢坐奠於兩楹，乃將亡之兆。蓋寢疾七日而沒。

此章所載，兼及孔子之葬禮。門人疑所服，子貢建議「若喪父而無服」，遂有公西赤為志之事：飾棺、牆置翣設披，周制；設崇，殷制；綢練設旐，夏制。兼三代之禮於一葬，反映魯國用天子禮樂之特殊地位。

其歌以泰山、梁木、哲人為喻，示天下失道之懼，亦聖人自知命盡之悟也。
