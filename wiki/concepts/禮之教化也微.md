content_legacy: '## English


  The *Book of Rites* articulates a philosophy of ritual as subtle transformative
  education. The text declares: **"故禮之教化也微，其止邪也於未形。使人日徙善遠罪而不自知也，是以先王隆之也。"** Ritual''s
  power lies not in explicit instruction but in imperceptible cultivation—it prevents
  wickedness before it takes form, causing people daily to move toward goodness and
  away from sin without their own awareness.


  This concept represents the Confucian ideal of *wei* (為) in education—that the most
  profound learning occurs without conscious effort. Like water flowing downward by
  nature, proper ritual environments naturally guide people toward virtue. The ancient
  kings esteemed ritual precisely because of this subtle efficacy: it accomplishes
  moral transformation through immersion rather than preaching.


  The concept connects to the famous *Yijing* quotation that concludes the passage:
  **"易曰：''君子慎始，差若豪厘，繆以千里。''"** Just as the smallest deviation at the outset leads
  to vast errors, the smallest attention to ritual at the beginning leads to profound
  moral development. Ritual thus serves as both preventive medicine for society and
  the foundation of the junzi''s self-cultivation.

《禮記·經解》提出禮之教化在乎微妙之說。孔子曰：「故禮之教化也微，其止邪也於未形。使人日徙善遠罪而不自知也，是以先王隆之也。」禮之功能，不在明言教誨，而在潛移默化；非待邪惡已成而後遏之，乃止之於未形。使人日日徙於善、遠於罪，而不自知其然。此聖王之所以隆禮之故也。

此說深合儒家無為而化之旨。蓋為政者以身教不以言教，置人於禮義之中而不設為科條，人自感化於不知不覺之間。禮之為教，猶春風之化雨，潤物細無聲；亦猶水之就下，本其自然之性而趨於善。

終以《易》言為結：「君子慎始，差若豪厘，繆以千里。」慎始之所以然，正緣微者之不可忽也。禮之於身，始於微而行於遠；禮之於國，始於教而成於治。微而大，斯之謂矣。
