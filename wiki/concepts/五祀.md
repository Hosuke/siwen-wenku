content_legacy: '## English


  The Five Sacrifices (五祀) constitute a system of localized domestic and community
  sacrifices performed at specific locations within the residence and neighborhood.
  Unlike the grand ancestral sacrifices conducted at designated altars, these sacrifices
  focus on protective spiritual forces dwelling in specific sites of the home and
  surrounding area.


  The five categories are: 司命 (Director of Fate), 中霤 (Central Gutter/Storm Spirit),
  國門 (Capital Gate), 國行 (Capital Road/God of Roads), and 泰厲/公厲/族厲 (Afflicted Ancestors
  of varying domains). Each spirit dwells at a particular location: the gate, the
  stove, the road, the gutter, or within the ancestral shrine.


  The Book of Rites establishes a graduated system of five sacrifices for kings, five
  for feudal lords, three for senior officials, two for properly ranked scholars,
  and one for common officers and commoners. The one permitted sacrifice for commoners
  could be either at the household door (戶) or at the stove (灶), indicating the most
  accessible and intimate spiritual protection available to ordinary people.


  This system demonstrates the Zhou conviction that spiritual protection operates
  at multiple scales—from the entire realm down to the individual household—and that
  each level of political authority carries corresponding religious responsibilities.

五祀者，祭祀於家內外之小神也。《禮記·祭法》記其制：

**天子七祀**：王為群姓立七祀，曰司命，曰中霤，曰國門，曰國行，曰泰厲，曰戶，曰灶。

**諸侯五祀**：諸侯為國立五祀，曰司命，曰中霤，曰國門，曰國行，曰公厲。

**大夫三祀**：大夫立三祀，曰族厲，曰門，曰行。

**士二祀**：適士立二祀，曰門，曰行。

**庶人一祀**：庶士庶人，立一祀，或立戶或立灶。

五祀之神，各有所司：司命主司人之命祿，中霤主堂室之風雨，國門主城門之守護，國行主道路之安寧，泰厲/公厲/族厲則為無後之鬼屬而為厲者。

鄭玄注云：「司命，主督察三元。」「中霤，主堂室居處。」「國門，城門也。」「國行，行神之位。」「泰厲，古代帝王無後者。」

此制以神之尊卑配人之貴賤，秩序井然，不可紊亂也。蓋祭祀之義，非特報功，亦所以祈福弭災、鎮護閭閻者也。
