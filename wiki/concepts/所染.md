  **On Being Affected by Associations** (所染) is a chapter from the Mozi (《墨子》) that
  employs the powerful metaphor of silk dyeing to illustrate how individuals—particularly
  rulers and scholars—are profoundly shaped by the company they keep and the influences
  they absorb.


  The chapter opens with Mozi observing silk being dyed: "染於蒼則蒼，染於黃則黃" (Dyed with
  blue, it becomes blue; dyed with yellow, it becomes yellow). Whatever color the
  silk enters, it takes on that color. This seemingly simple observation becomes the
  foundation for a sweeping meditation on moral and political causation.


  The central argument unfolds in three movements:


  First, Mozi applies the dye metaphor to governance. The sage kings—Shun, Yu, Tang,
  and Wu Wen—all achieved world dominion because they were "染於" (affected by) wise
  ministers: Xu You and Boyang guided Shun; Gao Yao and Bo Yi assisted Yu; Yi Yin
  and Zhonghui served Tang; Jiang Ziya and the Duke of Zhou supported Wu Wen. Their
  correct associations produced correct rule, earning them universal fame and lasting
  merit.


  In sharp contrast, the tyrant kings—Jie, Zhou, King Li, and King You—all perished
  in disgrace because they were染於 wicked counselors: Gan Xin and Tui Cha corrupted
  Jie; Duke of Wei and E Lai ensnared Zhou; Li Gong and Rong Yi troubled King Li;
  Bo Yi and Cai Gong damaged King You. Their improper染 led to national ruin, bodily
  destruction, and enduring shame.


  Second, Mozi extends this analysis to hegemon-rulers (霸). The five hegemons—Duke
  Huan of Qi, Duke Wen of Jin, King Zhuang of Chu, Helü of Wu, and King Goujian of
  Yue—all attained regional dominance through proper associations with worthy ministers.
  The six failed lords—Fan Jingshe, Zhonghang Yin, Wu Fuchai, Zhi Bo Yao, Zhongshan
  Shang, and King Kang of Song—all experienced state collapse and personal destruction
  because they associated with harmful figures.


  Third, and perhaps most significantly, Mozi applies the principle to scholars: "士亦有染"
  (Scholars too are affected by associations). A scholar whose friends are仁義 (humane
  and righteous), serious, and respectful will daily prosper in family, person, and
  reputation. A scholar whose friends are arrogant, contentious, and clannish will
  daily decline. Mozi concludes by quoting from the [[詩]] (Classic of Poetry): "必擇所堪，必謹所堪"
  (One must choose what one treads upon; one must be careful what one treads upon).


  The chapter demonstrates the Mohist concern with [[兼愛]] (universal love) and proper
  social relationships, while revealing a conservative view of social influence that
  would later inform Confucian thinking about friendship and moral cultivation.

**所染**者，《墨子》篇名也。墨子見染絲者而歎，以絲之染喻人之染，闡述人君與士人皆受所交所遊之浸染，染善則功成名立，染惡則國亡身戮。

篇首云：「染於蒼則蒼，染於黃則黃，所入者變，其色亦變。」墨子以為，人之受染，猶絲之受色，入蒼則蒼，入黃則黃，不可不愼。此喻既設，遂以論治國之道。

論治國，則以王天下者為證。四王者，舜、禹、湯、武是也。舜染於許由、伯陽，禹染於皋陶、伯益，湯染於伊尹、仲虺，武王染於太公、周公。此四王者所染當，故能王天下、立天子，功名蔽天地。天下稱仁義顯人，必稱此四王者。反是則有桀、紂、厲王、幽王。桀染於干辛、推哆，紂染於崇侯、惡來，厲王染於厲公長父、榮夷終，幽王染於傅公夷、蔡公穀。此四王者所染不當，故國殘身死，為天下僇。

論霸諸侯者，則有齊桓、晉文、楚莊、吳闔閭、越句踐。桓公染於管仲、鮑叔，晉文染於舅犯、高偃，楚莊染於孫叔、沈尹，吳闔閭染於伍員、文義，越句踐染於范蠡、大夫種。此五君者所染當，故霸諸侯，功名傳於後世。別有六君者——范吉射、中行寅、吳夫差、智伯搖、中山尚、宋康——染於不當之人，故國家殘亡，身為刑戮，宗廟破滅。

墨子進而言「士亦有染」：「其友皆好仁義，淳謹畏令，則家日益、身日安、名日榮」；「其友皆好矜奮，創作比周，則家日損、身日危、名日辱」。《詩》云「必擇所堪，必謹所堪」，正此之謂。

所染之義，非僅論交友而已，實涉[[墨學]]之核心——善惡之染出於所接之人，而人接善人則善，接惡人則惡。此與[[兼愛]]之說相通，亦見墨子對環境影響之重視。
