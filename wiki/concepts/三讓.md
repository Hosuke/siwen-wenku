content_legacy: '## English


  The **Threefold Yielding** (三讓) refers to the ritual practice in the village drinking
  ceremony where host and guest exchange three instances of deference before ascending
  to the ceremony hall. This practice exemplifies the virtue of yielding (讓) that
  characterizes proper ritual conduct.


  According to the text, the threefold yielding "resembles the moon taking three days
  to form its appearance" (讓之三也，象月之三日而成魄也). Just as the moon gradually reveals its
  luminous aspect, the participants in the ceremony gradually demonstrate their mutual
  respect through three successive gestures of yielding.


  The text connects this ritual to broader political philosophy by stating that "the
  three guests [三卿] are the fundamental basis of governmental instruction and the
  great trinity of ritual" (三賓者，政敎之本，禮之大參也). The number three thus becomes cosmologically
  significant, linking the microcosm of the ritual to the macrocosm of state governance.


  The threefold yielding, together with three bowings (三揖), constitutes the initial
  phase of the village drinking ceremony, establishing the tone of humility and mutual
  respect that pervades the entire ritual. This practice ensures that no participant
  approaches the ceremony with arrogance, thereby preventing the social discord that
  would arise from contempt and dispute.

**三讓**者，鄉飲酒禮中，賓主於升階前三度相讓之儀也。主人迎賓，三揖而後至階，三讓而後升。《鄉飲酒義》云：「讓之三也，象月之三日而成魄也。」

月之初三，光明始生；禮之三讓，敬意漸著。賓主相讓，非徒形式，乃心存謙遜之意也。蓋尊讓既行，則無爭奪；絜敬以事，則無怠慢。不爭不慢，則鬬辨遠而暴亂之禍免矣。

《記》又云：「三賓者，政敎之本，禮之大參也。」三讓與三卿相應，建國必立三卿，猶禮之有三讓。天地經之，日月紀之，三光參之，此禮樂之本也。

三揖三讓，為鄉飲之始，使與宴者皆知謙遜之義，無傲慢之心。是以禮行而爭端不起，序定而上下有序矣。
