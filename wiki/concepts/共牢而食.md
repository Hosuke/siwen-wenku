  "共牢而食" is a wedding ritual described in the *Book of Rites* (《禮記·昏義》) where the
  newlywed couple eats together from the same ceremonial offering (牢). The term "牢"
  refers to a sacrificial animal, typically a pig or a lamb, that is prepared as an
  offering during the wedding ceremony. The significance of this ritual lies in its
  symbolic meaning: by sharing the same food from the same vessel, the couple demonstrates
  their commitment to a life of equality and mutual support. According to the *Book
  of Rites*, this practice serves to "合體同尊卑以親之" — to unite their bodies and equalize
  their status in order to foster genuine affection between them. This ritual reflects
  the Confucian understanding of marriage as a partnership where husband and wife,
  though distinct in social function, share equally in the responsibilities and benefits
  of the household. The sharing of the "牢" also connects the private union of the
  couple to the broader cosmological and ritual order, as the "牢" is traditionally
  associated with sacrificial rites, linking the microcosm of the new family to the
  macrocosm of ancestral worship and social continuity.

共牢而食，昏禮中夫婦同食一牲之謂也。「牢」者，祭祀所用之牲也。昏禮之時，牲既陳，則夫婦共食之。《禮記·昏義》述昏禮之成，云：「共牢而食，合巹而酳；所以合體同尊卑以親之也。」言共牢之禮，所以使夫婦之恩合者也。夫食者，生民之本；共食者，合二人為一家之始也。牲為祭祀之物，用之昏禮，是婚娶之大事與祭祀祖宗相貫通也。一牲而夫婦共食之，是則二人共為一家之主人，同尊卑、共甘苦之義備矣。此禮行，而夫婦之義始立，家道之正始成。《儀禮·士昏禮》亦有「共牢而食」之文，是知此禮為士昏禮之定制，上自天子下至庶人皆用之，雖繁簡有差，而其義則一也。
