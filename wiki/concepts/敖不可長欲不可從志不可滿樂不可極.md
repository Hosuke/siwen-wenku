content_legacy: '## English


  **"敖不可長，欲不可從，志不可滿，樂不可極"** (Arrogance must not be fostered, desires must not be indulged,
  ambition must not be satisfied, pleasure must not reach its extreme) constitutes
  a fourfold admonition from the *Book of Rites*, establishing the Confucian approach
  to self-mastery and moral moderation.


  This teaching follows immediately after the famous opening of "Qu Li" which states:
  "毋不敬，儼若思，安定辭，安民哉" — "Let there be reverence, let there be thoughtful deliberation,
  let speech be measured — and the people will be settled."


  Together, these passages establish the Confucian moral program:


  1. **Positive cultivation** begins with reverence, thoughtfulness, and measured
  speech (the "three 安" — settle/shed security)

  2. **Negative precautions** warn against the four excesses that undermine moral
  progress


  The four dangers form pairs:

  - **Inner states**: Arrogance (敖) and Desires (欲) — mental/emotional excesses

  - **Outer manifestations**: Ambition (志) and Pleasure (樂) — action/experience excesses


  The principle of "不可" (must not) indicates these are tendencies natural to humanity
  that must be actively restrained, not transcended. This reflects the Confucian view
  that virtue requires continual effort against innate tendencies toward excess.


  **See also:** [[敖不可長欲不可從]], [[修身]], [[中庸-概論]], [[過猶不及]]

「敖不可長，欲不可從，志不可滿，樂不可極」十二字，出自《禮記·曲禮上》，為儒家修身之要訓。此四者皆人情所易趨，而聖人戒之者，正欲人防患於未然也。

敖者，矜伐自大之謂。人有才智，鮮有不驕者。驕則賢否莫辨，善惡不分，其害大矣。欲者，嗜欲無厭之謂。人之多欲，若水之就下，莫之能禦。從欲則肆，肆則行不義而無所不至。志者，希冀企慕之謂。志在功名，固君子之所當勉；然志得而滿，則或止步不前，或驕傲自是。樂者，歡娛宴享之謂。宴安鴆毒，人所易溺；樂極生悲，古之通誡。

此四者，其名異，其害同。要皆出於一念之不能節制也。聖人察其然，故特發此言，使學者知所戒謹。蓋[[修身]]之道，不特進於善而已，又當退損於不善；非獨有所作為，又當有所不作也。
