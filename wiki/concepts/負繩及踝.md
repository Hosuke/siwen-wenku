  The phrase **"負繩及踝"** (literally "the back cord reaching the ankle") describes one
  of the five symbolic elements embedded in the deep robe''s design. The seam running
  down the back of the robe, when suspended, extends straight to the ankle—this geometric
  line embodies the *cord* (*sheng*) used by craftsmen to ensure straightness and
  alignment.


  In the cosmological-ethical framework of the *Deep Robe* chapter, this physical
  feature carries profound meaning. The cord represents *straightness* and *integrity*,
  virtues essential to proper governance and personal cultivation. The text explicitly
  states: "The cord takes its image from straightness" (繩取其直). Just as the cord reveals
  any deviation from a true line, the moral person, adorned with the deep robe, should
  embody straightforward conduct without crookedness or deception.


  The philosophical underpinning draws from the *Yijing* tradition, where straightness
  and squareness are associated with the kun hexagram''s line statement: "The six
  second lines of Kun move directly and broadly" (坤六二之動，直以方也). This cosmological endorsement
  elevates the robe''s design from mere clothing regulation to a statement about the
  alignment between human action and cosmic order.


  The political implications are equally significant. The "straight" person (*zhi*)
  is one whose governance is not devious, whose policies follow direct paths, and
  whose administration does not circuitously burden the people. Thus, "bearing the
  cord to the ankle" becomes both a sartorial prescription and a moral-political ideal.

**負繩及踝**，語出《禮記·深衣》，言深衣背縫下垂至踝，取象繩之直也。

古者工匠以繩墨取直，審曲面勢，無有邪曲。深衣之背縫正直下垂，儷之以繩，示人服此衣者，當心存正直、行無邪曲也。《禮記》明言：「繩取其直。」直者，正也，不曲也。

其義與《易》相通。坤卦六二辭曰：「直以方也。」直者，內心之正也；方者，外行之事也。內直則外方，表裡如一，無有參差。聖人服深衣，取坤之象焉。

夫治國者亦然。為政以直，則政無邪枉；行法以公，則民不困惑。苟為政者心存邪曲，雖服用深衣，何益之有？故深衣之製，匪徒美觀，實以象德也。修德於身，行道於世，背縫之直，即心術之正也。
