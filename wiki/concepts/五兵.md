## English

The "Five Types of Military Action" (五兵, wǔ bīng) represents a sophisticated Daoist typology of military campaigns according to their ethical motivation and probable outcome. In the *Wenzi*, when Wen Zi inquires about the relationship between the Way and military force, Laozi provides this classification:

"誅暴救弱謂之義" — Military action to punish violence and rescue the weak is called **righteous war** (義兵).
"敵來加己不得已而用之謂之應" — Resisting an enemy's attack, using force only when there is no alternative, is called **responsive war** (應兵).
"爭小故不勝其心謂之忿" — Waging war over minor grievances, driven by inability to control anger, is called **wrathful war** (忿兵).
"利人土地，欲人財貨謂之貪" — Desiring another's territory and coveting their wealth is called **greedy war** (貪兵).
"恃其國家之大，矜其人民之眾，欲見賢於敵國者謂之驕" — Relying on one's nation's size, boasting of population, seeking to appear superior to rival states is called **arrogant war** (驕兵).

The outcomes follow predictably: "義兵王，應兵勝，恣兵敗，貪兵死，驕兵滅" (Righteous war leads to kingship; responsive war to victory; wrathful war to defeat; greedy war to destruction; arrogant war to annihilation). The text concludes: "此天道也" (This is the Way of Heaven).
