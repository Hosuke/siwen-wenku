  represents a sophisticated Daoist typology of military campaigns according to their
  ethical motivation and probable outcome. In the *Wenzi*, when Wen Zi inquires about
  the relationship between the Way and military force, Laozi provides this classification:\n\n\"誅暴救弱謂之義\"
  — Military action to punish violence and rescue the weak is called **righteous war**
  (義兵).\n\"敵來加己不得已而用之謂之應\" — Resisting an enemy's attack, using force only when there
  is no alternative, is called **responsive war** (應兵).\n\"爭小故不勝其心謂之忿\" — Waging war
  over minor grievances, driven by inability to control anger, is called **wrathful
  war** (忿兵).\n\"利人土地，欲人財貨謂之貪\" — Desiring another's territory and coveting their
  wealth is called **greedy war** (貪兵).\n\"恃其國家之大，矜其人民之眾，欲見賢於敵國者謂之驕\" — Relying on
  one's nation's size, boasting of population, seeking to appear superior to rival
  states is called **arrogant war** (驕兵).\n\nThe outcomes follow predictably: \"義兵王，應兵勝，恣兵敗，貪兵死，驕兵滅\"

  (This is the Way of Heaven).\n\nThis classification connects to the broader Daoist

**五兵**者，《節用上》所論軍事之器也。墨子言：「其為甲盾五兵何以為？以圉寇亂盜賊，若有寇亂盜賊，有甲盾五兵者勝，無者不勝，是故聖人作爲甲盾五兵。」

其取捨之道，仍以[[節用]]為準：凡為甲盾五兵，加輕以利、堅而難折者，則留之；不加者，則去之。用財不費，民德不勞，而興利多矣。

墨子之論五兵，非徒論兵器而已，乃以實用為衡，合則用之，不合則去。此亦[[聖人為政]]之要義也。
