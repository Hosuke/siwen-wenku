content_legacy: '## English


  The concept of "弗弗之臣" (frank remonstrating ministers) appears in Mozi''s "親士" chapter
  as an essential component of good governance. The text states: "君必有弗弗之臣，上必有詻詻之下"
  — the ruler must have ministers who are frank and dare to remonstrate, and the superior
  must have subordinates who speak boldly.


  Mozi''s concern here is with the danger of rulers becoming insulated from honest
  criticism. When "臣下重其爵位而不言，近臣則喑，遠臣則唫" — when subordinates value their positions
  and remain silent, when close ministers are mute and distant ministers are speechless
  — then "善議障塞，則國危矣" (good counsel is blocked, and the state is in danger). The text
  cites the examples of Jie and Zhou, who perished because they lacked worthy ministers
  who would speak the truth.


  The "弗弗" describes the attitude of ministers who dare to oppose and correct their
  rulers when they err. "詻詻" describes subordinates who speak boldly and forthrightly
  without fear. Together, these create an environment where "分議者延延，而支苟者詻詻" — where
  different opinions can be discussed at length and challenges to error can be voiced
  freely. Such an environment, Mozi argues, is essential for the long-term preservation
  of the state.


  This concept reflects Mozi''s practical approach to governance. Unlike Confucian
  emphasis on the ruler''s personal virtue as the primary means of good government,
  Mozi focuses on institutional mechanisms — the necessity of honest feedback from
  subordinates to keep the ruler''s power in check.

「弗弗之臣」之說，出自《墨子·親士》篇，為墨子論治國之要道。原文云：「君必有弗弗之臣，上必有詻詻之下。」

墨子此言，深憂人君蔽於近習、不知己過之弊。當「臣下重其爵位而不言，近臣則喑，遠臣則唫」之時，善議障塞，則國危矣。賢臣不言，諂諛在側，此亡國之兆也。

弗弗者，敢於犯顏直諫、拂逆君意之謂也。詻詻者，言論侃侃、無所畏懼之謂也。分議者延延，謂之異見可盡言；支苟者詻詻，謂之小人無所遁形。如此則下情上通，國可長保。

墨子引桀紂之亡以為殷鑑：非無天下之士，乃不能用之，殺其身而喪天下。可見弗弗之臣，不可一日而無。為人君者，當廣開言路，使直言之人樂於進諫，方為明主。此亦與墨子[[尚賢]]之旨相通——賢不在高居上位者，而在能糾君之過、救國之弊者。
