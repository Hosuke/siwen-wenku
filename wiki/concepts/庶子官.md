  The **Officer of Younger Sons** (庶子官, Shùzǐ Guān) represents a significant administrative
  office in the Zhou dynasty system of governance, documented in the *Yàn Yì* chapter
  of the *Book of Rites*. This official held responsibility for managing the younger
  sons (庶子) of all levels of nobility—from dukes and marquesses (諸侯) down to high
  ministers (卿), great officers (大夫), and junior officials (士).


  The term **庶子** in classical Chinese refers not to the biological "younger sons"
  in a modern sense, but to a formal category within the aristocratic household system.
  These were the sons of various ranks of officials and nobles who, while not inheriting
  their fathers'' primary positions, were nonetheless members of the ruling class
  and required proper education and governance to serve the state effectively.


  The duties of the 庶子官 encompassed several essential domains:


  **First, ritual instruction and governance**: The officer was responsible for issuing
  prohibitions and commands (戒令), overseeing the moral education (教治) of the younger
  sons, distinguishing their various ranks (別其等), and rectifying their proper positions
  (正其位). This educational function ensured that even those outside the primary line
  of succession would be properly formed as members of the governing class.


  **Second, military organization**: During times of national emergency (國有大事), the
  officer would lead the younger sons to the heir apparent (大子),，唯所用之—prepared to
  serve however their lord required. Additionally, when military campaigns demanded
  it, the officer would distribute chariots and armor, organize the troops into their
  proper formations (卒伍), appoint the appropriate officers (有司), and govern all according
  to martial law. The Minister of War (司馬), however, held supreme authority over military
  matters and could not be contradicted by the 庶子官 in such affairs.


  **Third, civil affairs**: When not engaged in military matters, the younger sons
  were assigned to various administrative duties (國子存游卒), where they were to cultivate
  virtue and learn the way of governance (使之德學道). Seasonal assemblies complemented
  this training: in spring, all gathered at the academic halls (學), and in autumn,
  they convened at the archery grounds (射), where their skills were examined and their
  advancement or demotion determined based on merit.


  This comprehensive system reveals the Zhou approach to cultivating governance capacity
  among the broader aristocratic class, ensuring that even those not destined for
  primary succession would serve the state effectively.

  庶子官（しょしのかん）は、周代において諸侯・卿・大師・士の庶子を管掌する官職である。『礼記・燕義』に詳しく記載される。

  庶子官の職務は広範にわたる。戒令の公布と教化（教治）を掌り、庶子の等位を区別し、その正しい位置を正す。国家有事際には国子を率いて太子に赴き、その指示に従った。軍事時は戦車・甲冑を配給し、卒伍を編成して有司を設置し、軍法によって統治した。ただし軍事最終権は司馬に属し、庶子官はこれに逆らえなかった。

庶子官者，周代掌理諸侯卿大夫士之庶子之官也。庶子非獨血統之幼子，乃大夫士諸侯之旁支，別於正嫡，以備他日之用者。

其職掌有數端：

**掌其戒令，與其教治**：庶子官掌理庶子之禁令教化，別其等第，正其位次，使各知其所當處。

**別其等，正其位**：分辨庶子之尊卑等級，糾正其朝位席次，使其明於尊卑之分。

**國有大事，則率國子而致於大子，唯所用之**：若國家有大事，則率領庶子前往太子之所，聽候調遣。

**甲兵之事**：若有軍事，則授以車甲，合其卒伍，置其有司，以軍法治之。然司馬弗正，軍法之事終決於司馬。

**國之政事**：國家有政事，庶子存游卒，使其德學道。春合諸學，秋合諸射，以考其藝而進退之。

蓋周人治世，重在培育人材，不拘一格。雖非嫡長，亦加教治，使各有以報效國家。此制之善，後世所當法也。
