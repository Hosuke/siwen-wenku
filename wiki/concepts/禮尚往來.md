content_legacy: '## English


  **"禮尚往來"** (Lǐ shàng wǎng lái), meaning "ritual emphasizes reciprocity," is a fundamental
  principle articulated in the *Book of Rites* (*Liji*), specifically in the "Qu Li
  Shang" (曲禮上, Upper Chapter on the Minor Rituals). The passage states: "禮尚往來：往而不來，非禮也；來而不往，亦非禮也"
  — "In ritual, reciprocity is valued: to give without receiving is not proper; to
  receive without giving is equally improper."


  This principle establishes the foundational framework of Confucian social ethics,
  wherein human relationships are maintained through balanced exchange. The concept
  implies that gifts must be reciprocated, kindness should be repaid, and social obligations
  flow in both directions. This creates a mutual accountability that binds society
  together.


  The principle extends beyond mere gift-giving to encompass all forms of social interaction.
  When one party offers respect, assistance, or hospitality, the other is obligated
  to respond in kind. Failure to reciprocate disrupts the harmony of social relations
  and violates the principle of *yi* (righteousness/propriety).


  This reciprocity operates at multiple levels: between individuals, within families,
  among friends, and between rulers and subjects. A ruler who bestows virtue upon
  the people expects loyalty in return; a teacher who imparts wisdom expects respect;
  a friend who offers aid expects gratitude.


  **See also:** [[禮-儒學-之]], [[禮者理也]], [[明於禮樂舉而錯之]]

「禮尚往來」出自《禮記·曲禮上》，為儒家倫理之重要原則。其言曰：「往而不來，非禮也；來而不往，亦非禮也。」此語明言：施受之間，當相報答，方為合禮。

夫禮之為用，非徒形式之末節，乃所以維繫人群之和諧者。往者施之，來者受之；受之者當有所報，施之者亦當有所還。一往一來，禮之樞機也。此所謂「禮尚往來」，非特指餽遺慶吊之際，凡交接應對之間，皆當以此為準。

儒家以為，人之異於禽獸者，在於知禮、知義、知報施之恩義。若有施無報，則是蔽於利而忘義；若受而不還，則是貪得無厭。二者皆非君子之所為也。

此理通於萬事：君恩臣忠，父慈子孝，師友切磋，賓主酬酢，莫不以此相維。[[禮-儒學-之]]之道，即以此為基礎，推而至於天下，則四海之內，皆可致太和。
