## English

"Superior Righteousness" (上義) represents the highest level of governance according to the *Wenzi*, categorized into three gradations. The text distinguishes: at the highest level, the ruler governs the state, regulates the realm, practices benevolence and righteousness, extends virtue and benefits, establishes correct laws, blocks evil ways, so that ministers and officials are closely attached, the people are harmonious, the court and populace share one heart, ministers and officials labor together, vassals submit to the ruler's prestige, and foreign lands embrace the ruler's virtue. The superior corrects what is within the ancestral temple, and without, defeats enemies a thousand miles away. When orders are issued, the world responds—such is the highest level.

At the second level: when the territory is vast and the people numerous, the lord is wise and the general capable, the state is wealthy and the army strong, discipline is maintained, orders are clear, two enemies face each other, yet before weapons clash, the enemy flees.

At the third level—the lowest: knowing the land's advantages, understanding strategic passes, clarifying harsh policies, examining battle formations, with blades meeting and arrows flying, carrying the dead and supporting the wounded, blood flowing a thousand miles, bones filling the wilderness—righteousness at its lowest.

The text emphasizes that military victory or defeat depends on political affairs. When politics conquer the people, the lower ones are attached to those above, and the army is strong. When the people overcome politics, the lower ones rebel against their superiors, and the army is weak. Superior righteousness possesses four sufficient conditions: its virtue is enough to win the hearts of the world's people; its enterprises are enough to meet the urgent needs of the world; its selection of talents is enough to obtain the support of worthy scholars; its strategic planning is enough to decide matters of weight and consequence.
