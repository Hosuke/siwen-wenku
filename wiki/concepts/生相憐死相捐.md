content_legacy: '## English


  **"Compassionate in Life, Releasing in Death"** (生相憐，死相捐) is a Yang Zhu doctrine
  from the *Liezi* encapsulating proper conduct toward the living and the dead respectively.
  The text declares this saying to be of the highest wisdom ("此語至矣").


  "Compassionate in life" (生相憐) encompasses not merely emotional sympathy but practical
  care: labor that relieves the weary, food that satisfies the hungry, warmth that
  shelters the cold, and aid that elevates the poor. Love for the living, in Yang
  Zhu''s view, must be embodied in material support and physical relief, not in verbal
  expressions or ritual performances alone.


  "Releasing in death" (死相捐) means that when the dead have passed, they should be
  set free without excessive ceremony. Yang Zhu lists what the dead do *not* require:
  pearls and jade in the mouth, brocaded garments, sacrificial animals, or ceremonial
  vessels. The funeral should involve no elaborate rites — neither burial objects
  (明器) nor elaborate coffins. Death is the ultimate release; the living should not
  bind the departed with their own grief or social display.


  This maxim stands in deliberate contrast to Confucian mourning rituals ([[三年之喪]])
  and the Mohist emphasis on inclusive love (兼愛) and funeral simplification. Yang
  Zhu accepts generous care for the living but rejects the elaborate mourning culture
  of the Zhou aristocratic tradition, viewing it as a burden imposed by the living
  upon themselves and upon the dead.


  The parallel dialogue between **Guan Yiwu** (管夷吾, i.e., Guan Gong) and **Yan Ying**
  (晏平仲) on the same subject reinforces this theme: Yan Ying asks Guan Yiwu about nourishing
  life (養生), and Yiwu instructs him to abandon all restraints ("肆之而已，勿壅勿閼"), to let
  the senses and desires run free. When asked about disposing of the dead, Yan Ying
  replies that burial is arbitrary — burning, sinking, earth burial, exposure, or
  a simple wood coffin are all equally valid. This passage establishes that Yang Zhu''s
  funeral practice is not a new ritual but the *absence* of ritual.

**生相憐，死相捐**者，《[[列子]]》[[楊朱]]篇所載之古語也。原文云：「古語有之：『生相憐，死相捐。』此語至矣。」楊朱以此為至理名言，開示世人待生待死之道。

所謂**生相憐**者，非徒情之感涕，實有事之周濟。凡勤能使逸，飢能使飽，寒能使溫，窮能使達，皆相憐之道也。楊朱以為愛人貴在實惠，不在虛言；當以物資養之，以力相助之，使生者得遂其欲，而不止於嗟嘆憐憫而已。

所謂**死相捐**者，言人死則已，當捐而去之，不宜復以生者之意縛之。其道如何？「不含珠玉，不服文錦，不陳犧牲，不設明器」——不納珠玉於口中，不衣錦繡以斂葬，不陳牛羊之祭品，不設平日所用之器物於壙中。葬禮從簡，棺槨衣薪，棄諸溝壑可也；盛服美棺，納諸石椁亦可也。葬法無定，唯在速遣，不在儀文。

此說與[[儒家]]隆喪之禮及[[墨家]]薄葬之論皆有別。[[楊朱]]非不重生人之愛，特以為死者已矣，不當以生人之哀情累之，不當以浮文縛之。死即解脫之時，不可復以生人之禮義強之。

[[管夷吾]]與[[晏平仲]]之問答可為旁證。晏平仲問養生之道，夷吾告以「肆之而已，勿壅勿閼」——放縱耳目口體之欲而無所遏止。問送死之道，平仲則以為「送死略矣」，燒之沈之瘞之露之皆可，葬法全無定式。[[楊朱]]之意，非欲立一新禮，乃欲去一切禮也。
