## English

The **Chief Steward** (宰夫, Zǎi Fū) was a significant official in the Zhou dynasty administrative hierarchy, primarily responsible for the ruler's food, drink, and ceremonial provisions. In the context of the banquet ritual (燕禮), the 宰夫 assumed the role of "offering host" (獻主), a function with profound implications for understanding the hierarchical structure of classical Chinese ritual.

The primary duty of the 宰夫 was to manage the ruler's **dietary and sacrificial offerings**. This encompassed everything from the daily meals to the special provisions required for state ceremonies. The position represented a delicate balance: while the 宰夫 handled matters intimately connected to the ruler's person, the very nature of the role required a specific form of subordination.

In the **燕禮** (Banquet Ritual), the 宰夫 serves as the 獻主 rather than the ruler's own ministers. This arrangement reflects a profound principle of ritual propriety: "no minister dares to be on equal terms with the ruler" (臣莫敢與君亢禮). If the high ministers (公卿) were to act as formal hosts for the banquet, this would suggest inappropriate equality with the sovereign. By substituting the 宰夫 in this role, the ritual preserves the unique dignity of the ruler's position.

The *Yàn Yì* explains this arrangement through the concept of **疑** (suspicion/doubt) and **嫌** (impropriety). When the ruler receives officials at a banquet, if high-ranking ministers were designated as the "guests" (賓), this would create the appearance that the ruler and his ministers were meeting as equals. The ritual therefore employs the following graduated distinctions:

1. The 宰夫 serves as the offering host, not the ruler's ministers
2. High ministers (公卿) are not made the formal guests
3. Senior officials (大夫) are received as guests instead

This graduated reception serves to **illuminate and clarify** (明) the proper distinctions, avoiding the appearance of inappropriate equality. The ritual, in other words, does not merely follow convention—it actively constructs and displays the moral-political order through every detail of spatial arrangement, role assignment, and procedural sequencing.

The position of 宰夫 thus exemplifies a key principle of classical Chinese political thought: the ruler's most intimate servants occupy the most public ritual roles precisely because their very intimacy would otherwise threaten the public hierarchy. By assigning the 宰夫 to serve as host, the ritual transforms what might seem like equality into an expression of hierarchy—the steward serves, the ruler reigns, and the ministers receive.

## 中文

宰夫者，周官之掌膳羞者也。膳夫之職，掌和王之食飲膳羞，以待頒賜。於燕禮之時，宰夫為獻主，代君行禮。

《燕義》云：「使宰夫為獻主，臣莫敢與君亢禮也。」此言宰夫之設，非偶然也。蓋君與臣之間，禮分尊卑，不可混亂。若使公卿為賓，則嫌於與君抗禮；若使大夫為賓，則可以明嫌之義矣。

是以燕禮之設，宰夫居中代君為主人，而大夫為賓，臣下莫敢與君亢禮。此正所以別尊卑、明貴賤者也。
