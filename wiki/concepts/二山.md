content_legacy: '## English


  **The Two Mountains** (二山) refer to two mystical locations described in *Baopuzi*
  as repositories of immortality secrets. These are not the famous sacred peaks of
  Huashan, Hengshan, Songshan, or Taiishan, but rather esoteric inner realms accessible
  through Taoist practice.


  The **太元之山** (Great Primordial Mountain) is characterized as:

  - "難知易求" — difficult to know yet easy to obtain

  - "不天不地，不沈不浮" — neither celestial nor terrestrial, neither sinking nor floating

  - "絕險綿邈，{山罪}嵬崎嶇" — of extreme danger, vast and distant, rugged and uneven

  - Within lies a divine realm with a jade well (玉井), a hundred and twenty officials
  (百二十官), herbs such as xuanzhi (玄芝) in ten thousand varieties, crimson trees (絳樹),
  gold and jade peaks (金玉嵯峨), and sweet springs (醴泉)


  The **長谷之山** (Long Valley Mountain) is characterized as:

  - "杳杳巍巍" — lofty and distant

  - "玄氣飄飄，玉液霏霏" — filled with mysterious energy and jade fluids

  - Contains a purple chamber (紫房) at its folds (隈)

  - Ordinary people who enter meet death, but those with the Dao ascend without decline

  - Consuming yellow essence (黃精) there leads to heavenly flight


  Both mountains represent states of cultivation rather than literal geographical
  locations. The text emphasizes that "皆古賢之所秘，子精思之" — these are secrets transmitted
  by ancient sages, requiring careful contemplation. The practice involves "採服黃精"
  (collecting and consuming golden saffron) for the Long Valley Mountain, while the
  Great Primordial Mountain emphasizes the comprehensive bureaucratic and cosmological
  ordering of its divine realm.


  See also: [[玄道]], [[真人]], [[學仙之法]].

**二山**，抱朴子所言可求長生之秘山，非世俗名山華霍、嵩岱之屬，乃道家內秘之境。

**太元之山**：難知易求，不天不地，不沈不浮。絕險綿邈，{山罪}嵬崎嶇。和氣絪縕，神意並游。中有玉井泓邃，灌溉匪休；百二十官曹府相由；離坎列位，玄芝萬株，絳樹特生；金玉嵯峨，醴泉出隅。還年之士挹其清流，修之則喬松可儔。

**長谷之山**：杳杳巍巍，玄氣飄飄，玉液霏霏。金池紫房，在乎其隈。愚人妄往，至皆死歸；有道之士，登之不衰，採服黃精以致天飛。

二山皆古賢所秘，當精思而修之。此為仙道修煉之象徵境界，非實指地理。相關概念見[[玄道]]、 [[真人]]、 [[學仙之法]]。
