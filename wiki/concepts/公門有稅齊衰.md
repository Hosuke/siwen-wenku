content_legacy: '## English


  **公門有稅齊衰** is a famous principle from the *Book of Rites* chapter 服問 concerning
  mourning dress in official settings.


  The passage states: "凡見人無免絰，雖朝於君，無免絰。唯公門有稅齊衰。《傳》曰：君子不奪人之喪，亦不可奪喪也。"


  This establishes that, as a general rule, one must not remove one''s mourning hempen
  headband (*免絰*) when visiting others, even when paying respects at court before
  the ruler. The mourning garment represents a state of ritual impurity and humility
  that should not be hastily set aside.


  However, there is a notable exception: at the gates of the duke''s palace (*公門*),
  one may "set aside" (*稅*) the *qi cui* (齊衰, a higher grade of mourning garment).
  This exception acknowledges the special sacrosanct nature of the ruler''s dwelling
  — the space is so elevated in ritual status that the mourning state must yield before
  it.


  The passage concludes with the *Zhuan* (傳, commentary) explaining this principle:
  "The gentleman does not strip others of their mourning, nor can he strip himself
  of his mourning." This captures the Confucian balance between social obligation
  and ritual propriety — one cannot arbitrarily abandon mourning any more than one
  can compel another to abandon theirs.


  This principle influenced later Chinese thought on the relationship between filial
  duty and political loyalty.

**公門有稅齊衰**，語出《禮記·服問》，為喪服禮制中著名之例外原則。

《傳》曰：「凡見人無免絰，雖朝於君，無免絰。唯公門有稅齊衰。」

此言凡往見他人，不可除去首絰；雖入朝見君，亦不可免絰。絰者，麻帶也，男子首絰、女子腰絰，皆表居喪之義，不可輕易除去。

然有一例外：至諸侯之門，則可稅齊衰。齊衰者，次於斬衰之重服也。蓋公門者，國之門庭，禮義所萃，不可使喪服之人以未除服之身而入焉。是以許其暫時解稅，以從吉禮而入也。

《傳》又曰：「君子不奪人之喪，亦不可奪喪也。」此言居喪者自持以禮，見之者亦不可強奪之也。彼此互相信守，喪禮乃得全也。

此制後世衍為「公門不稅」之說，蓋亦本此而推廣之。
