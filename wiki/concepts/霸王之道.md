## English

The "Way of Hegemon-Kings" (霸王之道) represents the philosophical framework for righteous warfare and legitimate rule as articulated in the *Wenzi*. According to this text, the hegemon's approach combines strategic planning with moral purpose—using wisdom to deliberate, policies to chart, and acting according to righteousness rather than for selfish preservation.

The core principle states: "The Way of hegemon-kings uses wisdom for deliberation, strategy for planning, and moves according to righteousness, not to secure survival, but to preserve the dying and rescue the perishing." This distinguishes legitimate military action from mere aggression. When encountering a hostile ruler who oppresses the people, the righteous leader mobilizes forces to intervene, denouncing the ruler's injustice and exposing their faults.

Upon arrival, strict military discipline is maintained: no felling of trees, no excavation of graves, no destruction of crops, no burning of stockpiles, no capture of civilians, no slaughter of livestock. The proclamation declares the enemy ruler guilty of "going against Heaven and Earth, insulting the gods and spirits, rendering unfair judgments, and slaughtering the innocent"—thus becoming an object of heavenly punishment and popular vengeance.

The key distinction lies in the moral framework: "A righteous army arrives at the border but stops without fighting; an unrighteous army creates corpses and bloodshed." The hegemon wages war to eliminate unrighteousness and bestow virtue, not to conquer or dominate. Those who fight for territorial gain cannot achieve kingship; those who seek personal advantage cannot establish merit. Only those who act for the sake of others receive the support of the many.
