content_legacy: '## English


  The term **道家** (Dàojiā, literally "School of the Way") refers to an ancient Chinese
  philosophical school centered on the concept of 道 (the Way/Dao). Emerging in the
  Warring States period, Daoism emphasizes following nature, governing through non-action
  (無為而治), and allowing people to fulfill their innate nature (各遂其性). The school stands
  in deliberate contrast to [[儒-儒學-之|Confucianism]], which prioritizes external rituals,
  moral teaching, and active governance. Daoist thinkers, such as those found in the
  *Zhuangzi* and *Liezi*, argue that excessive moral instruction and ritual constraint
  obscure the natural spontaneity of the people. Rather than imposing order through
  the sage-ruler''s teachings, the Daoist ideal envisions a society where individuals
  live in harmony with the Dao, free from artificial constraints and governmental
  interference. The school''s philosophy also encompasses inner cultivation—a turning
  inward to stillness and self-reflection (虛靜), rejecting both the pursuit of self-interest
  and the restless anxiety to transform the world.

**道家**，先秦時期重要思想流派，以「道」為核心概念。[[華胥之夢]]篇載其要義，以為道家之要，在於使民各遂其性，而非聖人強行教化。[[痀僂承蜩]]篇又記道家對[[儒-儒學-之|儒學]]之批判，以為世儒但知外在之禮儀，不知內在之工夫。其政治理想為無為而治，退而齋心，去欲去智，復歸虛靜，使民安其性，不知有治者。黃帝之悟道，即始於自反——知「養一己」與「治萬物」皆為過淫之患，乃退而齋心。此與儒家堯舜之治及《大學》所言「明明德」「新民」不同其途。[[造父學御]]所謂「執轡若掘」，[[知幻化之不異生死]]所言之「心凝形釋」，皆道家心志凝注之意也。

《要略》論道與言之本末曰：「道論至深，故多為之辭，以抒其情；萬物至眾，故博為之說，以通其意。辭雖壇卷連漫，絞紛遠緩，所以洮汰滌蕩至意，使之無凝竭底滯，卷握而不散也。」

  此[[帝王之術]]所以[[帝王之道|達道]]之方，而[[言道而不言事|言道不言事]]之戒亦在其中矣。
