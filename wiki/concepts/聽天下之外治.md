content_legacy: '## English


  "聽天下之外治" (governing the external affairs of the realm) refers to the emperor''s
  administrative responsibilities in the Confucian political system. According to
  the *Book of Rites* (《禮記·昏義》), the emperor establishes six ministries (六官) — comprising
  three high officials (三公), nine ministers (九卿), twenty-seven senior officials (二十七大夫),
  and eighty-one junior officials (八十一元士) — to manage the external affairs of the
  realm. This external governance encompasses the administration of the state, the
  management of military affairs, the oversight of officials, and the implementation
  of policies that affect the broader society. The text states that by fulfilling
  this role, the emperor ensures "外和而國治" — harmony in external relations and proper
  governance of the state. The doctrine of "外治" stands in complementary opposition
  to "內治" (internal governance), which falls under the empress''s jurisdiction. The
  *Book of Rites* articulates this complementary relationship: "天子聽男教，后聽女順；天子理陽道，后治陰德；天子聽外治，后聽內職。教順成俗，外內和順，國家理治。"
  This passage establishes the fundamental principle that the emperor governs the
  "yang" (陽道) affairs of the realm — representing the masculine, external, and paternal
  aspects of governance — while the empress governs the "yin" (陰德) affairs — representing
  the feminine, internal, and maternal aspects. Together, these complementary spheres
  of governance create the conditions for "盛德" (great virtue) and proper social order.
  The concept also connects to the cosmological principle that the sun (associated
  with the emperor) and the moon (associated with the empress) depend upon each other,
  just as the male and female principles are mutually constitutive.

聽天下之外治，乃天子之職責也。《禮記·昏義》述天子之建置云：「天子立六官、三公、九卿、二十七大夫、八十一元士，以聽天下之外治，以明章天下之男教；故外和而國治。」言天子率公卿大夫元士，以治邦國之事也。外治之事，非徒治國而已，乃所以正天下之男者也。治之如何？明章男教，使天下之為人子、為人臣、為人士者，皆知所以盡其孝弟忠順之道，此天子之責也。外治本乎男教，男教明而後臣正、士端，邦國治而天下平。是故外治之與內治，實相須而成者也。《昏義》又云：「天子聽男教，后聽女順；天子理陽道，后治陰德；天子聽外治，后聽內職。教順成俗，外內和順，國家理治。此之謂盛德。」陽道發於外，陰德成於內；外治者，天之道也；內治者，地之道也。天地之道全，而治道之極致至矣。
