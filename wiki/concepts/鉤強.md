  In this passage from *Mozi·Lu Wen*, the debate between Gongshongzi (公輸子) and Mozi
  addresses naval warfare and the nature of true advantage. Gongshongzi, having invented
  hook and strength devices (鉤強) for ship combat that entangle retreating vessels
  and repel advancing ones, asks Mozi whether his doctrine of Righteousness (義) also
  possesses such devices.


  Mozi responds that his "hook of righteousness" surpasses mechanical hooks in power.
  His hooks are forged through mutual love (愛), and his "strength" is exercised through
  respectful conduct (恭). Without love, hooks fail to create genuine bonds; without
  respectful conduct, interactions become familiarity without intimacy, leading to
  swift separation. Thus, mutual love and mutual respect achieve mutual benefit.


  In contrast, Gongshongzi''s mechanical hooks entangle opponents who entangle you;
  strength repels those who repel you. Mutual hooking and mutual strength become mutual
  harm. Mozi concludes that his "hook of righteousness" excels over Gongshongzi''s
  naval hooks, establishing that in Mohist philosophy, moral instruments of unity
  transcend mere mechanical devices of force.

《墨子·魯問》載公輸子與墨子論「鉤強」之事。公輸子為楚國造舟戰之器，鉤退者、強進者，欲以此敗越。公輸子自矜其巧，問墨子：「我舟戰有鉤強，不知子之義亦有鉤強乎？」

墨子答以「義之鉤強，賢於子舟戰之鉤強」。墨子之鉤，以愛行之；墨子之強，以恭行之。不以愛鉤則不親，不以恭強則速狎。狎而不親，則速離。故交相愛、交相恭，猶若相利。

公輸子之鉤，止人亦為人所止；其強，距人亦為人所距。交相鉤、交相強，猶若相害。此闡明墨學之要義：[[兼愛]]非徒為機械之力，乃以仁愛為鉤、恭敬為強，使天下相親相爱、相利相恭，異於以力服人、以強凌弱之術也。
