content_legacy: '## English


  The concept of **哭踊有節** (regulated mourning crying and foot-stamping) emerges from
  *Liji · Tan Gong Shang* (禮記·檀弓上) as a fundamental principle governing proper mourning
  conduct. When the Master observed a mourner weeping excessively like a child, Confucius
  remarked: "哀則哀矣，而難為繼也。夫禮，為可傳也，為可繼也。故哭踊有節。" — "Grief may be deep, but it must be
  sustainable. Ritual exists to be transmitted and continued. Therefore, crying and
  foot-stamping must be measured."


  This principle reflects the Confucian balancing of emotional expression with social
  continuity. While genuine grief is natural, excessive display would render the rituals
  impossible to maintain over the extended mourning period. The regulations ensure
  that mourning practices remain practicable for all participants across the prescribed
  timeframe, whether for parents, siblings, or other relations.


  The concept connects to broader discussions in the *Liji* regarding the calibration
  of ritual expression, where Confucius repeatedly advocated moderation — neither
  insufficient emotion nor overwhelming display. See also [[拜而後稽顙]] for the ordering
  of mourning bow-kowtow sequences.

「哭踊有節」出自《禮記·檀弓上》，為喪禮中哭泣頓足之節制原則。文中記載：

「弁人有其母死而孺子泣者，孔子曰：『哀則哀矣，而難為繼也。夫禮，為可傳也，為可繼也。故哭踊有節。』」

孔子評論此事，謂：「哀痛之情雖真，然難以為繼。禮之為物，在於可傳、可繼，故哭踊必當有節。」

此乃儒家論喪禮之要義：情貴真摯，然亦需節制有度，使禮儀可持久而行。蓋三年之喪為時甚長，若哀毀過甚，則儀節無以為繼。此與《論語》所記「哀而不傷」之旨相通。[[三年之喪]]、 [[天下之達喪]] 皆依此節制之義而立。
