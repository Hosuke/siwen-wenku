content_legacy: '## English


  The concept of "世子齒學" (Heir Apparent Studying by Age) is found in the *Liji* (Book
  of Rites), *Wenwang Shizi* (King Wen as Heir Apparent). The text states: "行一物而三善皆得者，唯世子而已。其齒於學之謂也。"
  This means that when the heir apparent studies among others at the school, he shares
  meals and sits according to age rather than rank, thereby demonstrating the principles
  of filial piety among father and son, and proper order among elders and juniors.


  The text elaborates on how the people observe the heir apparent: "故世子齒於學，國人觀之曰：『將君我而與我齒讓何也？』曰：『有父在則禮然，然而眾知父子之道矣。』其二曰：『將君我而與我齒讓何也？』曰：『有君在則禮然，然而眾著於君臣之義也。』其三曰：『將君我而與我齒讓何也？』曰：『長長也，然而眾知長幼之節矣。』"


  This practice yields three benefits: it teaches the people the Way of father and
  son, clarifies the righteousness between ruler and minister, and demonstrates the
  proper order between elders and juniors. Thus, the text concludes: "故學之為父子焉，學之為君臣焉，學之為長幼焉，父子、君臣、長幼之道得，而國治。"


  The *Zhouli* (Rites of Zhou) records the office of "司儀" (Director of Ceremonial)
  with the instruction "掌擯相之事" (overseeing the arrangements for receiving guests),
  which relates to the formalization of these age-based protocols.


  The phrase "樂正司業，父師司成，一有元良，萬國以貞" also appears, emphasizing that when the music official
  oversees the transmission of learning and the father-teacher oversees the completion
  of virtue, the one truly good person (the heir apparent) brings stability to all
  under heaven.

《禮記·文王世子》載世子齒學之義。其文云：「行一物而三善皆得者，唯世子而已。其齒於學之謂也。」蓋世子入學，不以尊位自居，而與國人齒讓相待，是以父子的道理由此明知，長幼的秩序由此可見。

國人觀之而疑曰：「將君我而與我齒讓何也？」答曰：「有父在則禮然，然而眾知父子之道矣。」又問：「有君在則禮然，然而眾著於君臣之義也。」又問：「長長也，然而眾知長幼之節矣。」

是故父子、君臣、長幼三道，皆由世子齒學而得昭明於天下。[[學之為父子]]，[[學之為君臣]]，[[學之為長幼]]，莫非此義。[[樂正司業]]，[[父師司成]]，[[一有元良]]，萬國以貞，此之謂也。
