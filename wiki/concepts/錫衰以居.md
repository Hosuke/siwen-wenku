  The term **錫衰以居** (hair-cloth mourning worn during official residence) appears in
  the *Book of Rites* chapter 服問, establishing specific regulations for how officials
  should observe mourning while maintaining their administrative duties.


  The passage states: "公為卿大夫錫衰以居，出亦如之。當事則弁絰。大夫相為，亦然。為其妻，往則服之，出則否。"


  This regulation addresses the practical dilemma faced by officials who must both
  observe proper mourning and fulfill their governmental responsibilities. The ruling
  permits a duke or the ruler to wear *xi cui* (錫衰, hair-cloth garments of coarser
  quality than full mourning) while conducting routine administrative affairs at home.
  The same garment is worn when going out, except in specific ritual circumstances.


  When actually attending a funeral or engaging in mourning rites (*當事*), the official
  must change to *bian ji* (弁絰) — ceremonial caps with hemp bands — the full dress
  appropriate to active mourning service. The rule extends to interactions between
  high officials (*大夫相為*) who observe the same protocol.


  Notably, for one''s own wife, the rule simplifies: mourning is observed when going
  *to* the funeral (*往*), but not required when leaving or returning (*出*).


  This passage reveals the sophisticated balancing of ritual purity and governmental
  necessity in classical Confucian statesmanship.

**錫衰以居**，出自《禮記·服問》，詳定諸侯卿大夫居喪服制之禮。

《傳》曰：「公為卿大夫錫衰以居，出亦如之。當事則弁絰。大夫相為，亦然。為其妻，往則服之，出則否。」

此言諸侯為卿大夫服錫衰，居家聽事皆服之。錫衰者，粗麻布所製之衰，比之正喪服為稍輕。蓋諸侯於卿大夫之喪，不若父子之親，故服錫衰以示別，而不廢國事也。

「當事則弁絰」者，謂親臨其事——如會葬、奠祭之時——則易以弁冠加絰，此為居喪之正服。「大夫相為，亦然」，謂大夫與大夫之間，亦循此例。

至於為妻服喪，則又有別：「往則服之，出則否」。蓋往者，親赴喪事之謂也；出者，歸而即吉之謂也。妻之喪輕於臣屬，故規制亦稍略。

此制昭示居喪之禮非一成不變，而貴乎因時制宜、酌情定禮之妙用也。
