## English

**Food: The Root of the People** (食者人之本) is a fundamental governance principle from the Wenzi asserting that food is the foundation of human existence, and thus agriculture must be the priority of any legitimate government.

The text states: "Food is the root of the people; the people are the foundation of the state. Therefore, when a ruler governs, he must utilize the timing of heaven above, exhaust the benefits of the earth below, and employ human labor in between. Thus all living things grow and flourish, all species reproduce" (食者人之本也，民者國之基也，故人君者，上因天時，下盡地理，中用人力。是以群生遂長，萬物蕃殖).

This principle leads to concrete policies of sustainable resource management: in spring, cut only the withered; in summer, gather all fruits; in autumn, store vegetables; in winter, collect firewood. The former kings' laws forbade fishing with large nets, draining marshes, or hunting with fire during forbidden seasons. They protected the breeding grounds of animals—before the otter's sacrifice offering, nets could not enter the water; before the falcon's sacrifice offering, traps could not be set; before vegetation fell, axes could not enter the forest; before insects hibernated, burning fields was prohibited. They refrained from hunting pregnant animals, taking eggs, or catching fish less than a foot long or piglets before one year old.

The text concludes: "When the hundred affairs follow the timing of heaven, this is the way to enrich the state and benefit the people. Not what the eyes see and the feet walk, but the heart that wishes to benefit the people will not forget the people" (先王之所以應時脩備，富國利民之道也，非目見而足行之，欲利民者也不忘乎心，即人自備矣).

## 中文

**食者人之本**出自《[[文子]]》，闡述食乃民生之本、民乃國之基，此乃治國之根本大法。
