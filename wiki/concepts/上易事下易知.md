content_legacy: '## English


  The opening passage of the Chapter on Black Robes (緇衣) in the Book of Rites presents
  a fundamental principle of governance: "子言之曰：爲上易事也，爲下易知也，則刑不煩矣。"


  This aphorism establishes a reciprocal relationship between ruler and subjects.
  When those in positions of authority conduct themselves with simplicity and directness
  (易事), their governance becomes effortless. Correspondingly, when those below are
  treated with transparency and clarity, they develop the intelligence to discern
  right from wrong (易知). The natural consequence is that punitive measures become
  unnecessary—"刑不煩" (punishment need not be troublesome).


  The passage reflects the Confucian view that moral governance depends less on legal
  enforcement than on the exemplary conduct of superiors. When the ruler''s character
  is transparent and his commands reasonable, the people naturally respond with loyalty
  and understanding, rendering elaborate systems of reward and punishment superfluous.


  This principle connects to the broader teaching that the ruler serves as the "tabled
  of the people" (民之表), as developed further in the same chapter.

《禮記·緇衣》首章云：「子言之曰：爲上易事也，爲下易知也，則刑不煩矣。」

此語闡明治道之要義：居上位者行事簡易明白，則臣民易於理解服從，刑罰自然無所用之。蓋儒家以為，治國之本不在刑賞，而在君上之德行感召。上若誠信簡易，下則聰明易知；上下之間以心相感，以德相化，則刑罰無需矣。

此理與「堯禪天下」、「無為而治」之說相通，皆主以德化人、以身作則之旨。
