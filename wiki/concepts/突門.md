content_legacy: '## English


  **Hidden Gate** (突門) refers to a concealed gate built into city walls at regular
  intervals for defensive purposes. According to the *Mozi* chapter "Defending Against
  Hidden Gates" (備突), these gates should be constructed every hundred paces along
  the wall.


  The strategic purpose of hidden gates was dual: first, they allowed defending forces
  to make sudden sallies against besieging enemies without exposing the main gates;
  second, they could serve as decoy points of vulnerability. Because these gates were
  concealed within the wall structure, they could be exploited by attacking forces
  if not properly defended. The text specifies that each hidden gate must be equipped
  with kiln stoves (窖竈) positioned four to five chi inside the tunnel entrance, with
  tile roofs (瓦屋) to prevent water ingress.


  The hidden gate represents a sophisticated understanding of urban military architecture
  in ancient China. By distributing concealed exits along the wall, defenders could
  respond rapidly to enemy attacks at any point while maintaining the overall defensive
  integrity of the fortification.


  This concept connects to broader studies of [[守圍城之法]] (principles of defending besieged
  cities), [[城上之備]] (wall defenses), and the specialized defensive measures described
  in [[行城襍樓]] (mobile walls and multi-tiered defenses).

**突門**，城牆上每隔百步所設之隱蔽門戶也。見於《墨子》〈備突〉篇。

突門之設，用意有二：其一，便於守軍潛師出擊，襲擊圍城之敵，免暴露正門；其二，可設伏兵，使敵誤判城防之虛實。然而此門藏於牆中，若無戒備，反可為敵人所乘。《墨子》云：「突門各為窖竈」，謂每門皆當置窖竈於門內四五尺處，「門上瓦屋」以防水浸。

由此可見古代城邑建造之巧思：分散設置隱蔽出口，使守軍得以迅速應對牆垣任一點之攻勢，而仍保持城池之整體防禦功能。突門之制，與[[守城之十四者]]所論之城防要務相表裡，亦見於[[備城門]]篇所述之門守技法。
