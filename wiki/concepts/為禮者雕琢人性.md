## English

The concept of **為禮者雕琢人性** presents Wenzi's philosophical critique of ritual (禮) as an artificial imposition upon natural human nature. This passage represents a distinctly Daoist perspective that contrasts sharply with Confucian valorization of ritual propriety.

According to this text, those who administer ritual engage in "雕琢人性"—carving and polishing human nature. Ritual restrains the eyes from what they desire through prohibition, limits the heart's joy through propriety, demands elaborate postures and circling movements, bows and bends in lowly submission. The body is constrained, the inner virtue troubled, the harmony of yin and yang disrupted, and the genuine emotions of life suppressed.

The result, Wenzi argues, is that those who devotedly practice ritual become "哀人"—people of perpetual sorrow throughout their lives. Why? Because they are forbidden from satisfying their desires without addressing the source of those desires, and they attempt to prevent joy without investigating why joy arises. This is likened to enclosing a wild beast without blocking its escape, or trying to dam a great river with one's hands.

The text employs the famous Daoist paradox from the *Tao Te Ching*: "開其兌，濟其事，終身不救"—open the openings and complete the affairs, and nothing can save them throughout their lives. The proper approach, Wenzi suggests, is not to prevent people from wanting or enjoying, but to address the underlying causes.

Wenzi then poses a rhetorical question that cuts to the heart of the matter: "夫使天下畏刑而不敢盜竊，豈若使無有盜心哉！" — Is it not better to eliminate the thief's heart entirely than to make the world fear punishment?

The conclusion draws an analogy: a person who knows that winter fans and summer fur coats are useless to themselves will see all things transform into dust and dirt. This illustrates the Daoist insight that artificial constraints ultimately fail—the only true solution is to address the nature of desire itself.

## 中文

「為禮者雕琢人性」乃文子對禮之批判，以為禮制乃雕琢、磨礪人性，非自然之道也。

文子以為：「為禮者雕琢人性，矯拂其情。」禮之為用，使「目雖欲之禁以度，心雖樂之節以禮，趣翔周旋，屈節卑拜」。外在則束縛身形，內在則愁擾德性，陰陽之和被迫，性命之情受抑。

如此之人，終身為哀人，無有喜樂。何也？「不本其所以欲，而禁其所欲，不原其所以樂，而防其所樂。」此譬如圈獸而不塞其垣，禁其野心，決江河之流而壅之以手。

文子引老子之言：「開其兌，濟其事，終身不救。」欲開其源而塞其流，終不可得。

其論禮樂之用曰：「禮者，遏情閉欲，以義自防，雖情心㖥噎，形性飢渴，以不得已自強，故莫能終其天年。」

又設問以明其旨：「夫使天下畏刑而不敢盜竊，豈若使無有盜心哉！」禮法之防，不若去其欲盜之心。此與儒家以禮防民者，截然異趣。

## 日本語

**為禮者雕琢人性**とは、文子における礼への哲学的批判である。礼は自然の人性を人工的に制約するものであると論じる。

礼を行う者は「人性を雕琢」する。礼は目を禁じ心を節し、丁寧な動作を要求し身体を拘束する。これにより陰陽の調和が乱れ、生命の真情が抑圧される。

文子によれば、このように礼に専念する者は一生涯「哀れな人」となる。単に欲望を禁止し喜びを防ぐだけでは足りない。真の原因に対処すべきである。

これは野獸を囲むがその垣を塞がず、大きな川を堰き止めようとして手を用いるようなものである。老子の言葉を引く：「その竇を開けば、その事を済ます。一生救われず。」

最も重要な問いかけ：「天下をして刑を畏れて盗竊不敢にせんは、豈かにして盗心无からしむに若かまんや！」刑罰で畏怖させるより、盗みを欲する心をなくす方が勝れている。
