  The concept of **天之瑞令** (tiān zhī ruì lìng) — the celestial auspicious command or
  divine mandate — appears prominently in Mozi''s historical narratives of the three
  sage kings'' campaigns. This term refers to the heavenly signs and cosmic portents
  that authorize the sage king to undertake punitive action against a depraved ruler.


  In the narrative of Yu''s campaign against the Youmiao, Mozi describes how Gao Yang
  (高陽, the posthumous name of Emperor Zhuanxu) commanded Yu in the Xuan Palace (玄宮),
  and Yu personally grasped the celestial auspicious command (天之瑞令) to march against
  Youmiao. This command was authenticated by supernatural signs: thunder and lightning
  served as guides, and a deity with a human face and bird''s body (人面鳥身) held a jade
  ornament and guarded with a bow, confirming the divine authorization.


  Similarly, when Tang received the great mandate (大命) to overthrow Xia, the text
  describes how the Supreme Deity (帝) commanded him from the Biao Palace (鑣宮), stating
  that Xia''s virtue had become thoroughly disordered. The deity promised to enable
  Tang to "thoroughly subdue" (大堪) the Xia, and subsequently sent the fire deity Rong
  (融) to burn Xia''s cities.


  The concept serves Mozi''s broader argument that the sage kings'' military actions
  were not aggressive conquest but heavenly mandated punishment. The celestial signs
  — portents, divine messages, and supernatural assistance — legitimized what would
  otherwise appear as ordinary warfare.

「天之瑞令」乃《墨子·非攻下》所載之重要概念，指天命降臨、祥瑞顯現，以授權聖王行征誅之事。

其文云：「高陽乃命玄宮，禹親把天之瑞令，以征有苗。」言禹受天命，手持瑞令，遂征有苗。又有神人相助，「四電誘祗，有神人面鳥身，若瑾以侍，搤矢有苗之祥」，此皆天意昭示之兆。

湯之伐桀亦然。天帝「命湯於鑣宮，用受夏之大命」，明示夏德大亂、天命已絕，令湯往誅之。天帝又遣火神融，「隆火於夏之城閒西北之隅」，以助湯成功。

武王伐紂之時，赤烏銜珪，降於岐社，天命周文王伐殷。泰顛來賓，河出綠圖，地出乘黃，皆天瑞之象。

天之瑞令之要義：聖王非以私意興師，乃奉天之命、行天之罰者也。瑞令者，天人相感之媒介，聖王得之則師出有名，征伐有據。
