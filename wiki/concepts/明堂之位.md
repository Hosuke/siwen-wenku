content_legacy: '## English


  The "Positions at the Bright Hall" (明堂之位) constitutes one of the most systematic
  descriptions of ritual hierarchy in classical Chinese texts. According to the *Liji*
  (Book of Rites), the Duke of Zhou convened an audience of feudal lords at the Bright
  Hall in the sixth year of his regency, establishing a precise spatial ordering that
  reflected the cosmic and political order.


  The arrangement placed the Son of Heaven (天子) facing south, seated upon the斧依 (axe-shaped
  screen), with the Three Ministers (三公) positioned before the central staircase,
  facing north with eastern positions taking precedence. The hierarchy continued through
  the various ranks: feudal lords (諸侯) east of the main staircase facing west, the
  Earls (諸伯) west of the western staircase facing east, sons of the ruler (諸子) east
  of the gate facing north, and barons (諸男) west of the gate facing north.


  The four categories of barbarian rulers (夷蠻戎狄) were assigned positions outside the
  gates according to their cardinal direction: the Nine Yi (九夷) to the east, the Eight
  Man (八蠻) to the south, the Six Rong (六戎) to the west, and the Five Di (五狄) to the
  north. The Nine Cai (九采) lords were positioned outside the Ying Gate.


  The text explicitly states: "The Bright Hall exists to manifest the honor and humility
  of the feudal lords" (明堂也者，明諸侯之尊卑也). This formulation reveals that spatial positioning
  was not merely decorative but constituted a primary mechanism for expressing and
  reinforcing political hierarchy. The Bright Hall functioned as a microcosm of the
  ordered world, where the alignment of human positions reflected the cosmic regularity
  maintained by the Son of Heaven.


  The principle of "facing north for eastern precedence" (北面東上) appears repeatedly,
  indicating that the eastern position—associated with the rising sun and spring—held
  precedence over western positions. This reflects the broader cosmological logic
  that governed Zhou ritual systems, where directional symbolism served as the foundation
  for social and political ordering.

「明堂之位」見於《禮記·明堂位》，詳述周公踐天子之位後，於明堂朝會諸侯時所立之方位秩序。此制非徒空間排列而已，實為政治秩序之空間化呈現，體現天人合一之宇宙觀念。

其位次之要義如下：天子負斧依南鄉而立，位於最高之尊位。三公立於中階之前，北面東上，取向明之義。諸侯立於阼階之東，西面北上。諸伯之國立於西階之西，東面北上。諸子之國立於門東，北面東上。諸男之國立於門西，北面東上。

四夷之位則依其方位分之：九夷之國在東門之外，西面北上；八蠻之國在南門之外，北面東上；六戎之國在西門之外，東面南上；五狄之國在北門之外，南面東上。九采之國在應門之外，北面東上。四塞之國則世告至而已，不與常朝之列。

鄭玄注云：「明堂者，貫通三統也。」明堂之制，上承天陽，下接地陰，中治人道，乃天子布政之宮。其方位之設，依陰陽之氣、六合之位而立，以明天子為天地人物之主宰。諸侯按其封爵之高下，各有其位；夷狄蠻戎雖非華夏，亦各有其所寓之位，顯示周朝禮制之包容性與秩序性。

《禮記正義》言：「明堂之位，明諸侯之尊卑者，位既定，則上下之分嚴，內外之別異。」此位次之義，不在方位的本身，而在於透過空間之安排，以見政治秩序之倫常。
