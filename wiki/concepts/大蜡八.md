  The **Eightfold Year-End Sacrifice** (大蜡八) represents the most elaborate sacrificial
  ceremony in ancient Chinese ritual practice, as described in the "Record of Rites"
  (《禮記》), specifically in the chapter "Jiao Te Sheng" (郊特牲). This ceremony, established
  by the legendary ruler Yi Qizhi (伊耆氏), embodies the agricultural calendar''s culmination
  and expresses humanity''s gratitude to the forces that sustain cultivation.


  The chapter opens by stating: "天子大蜡八。伊耆氏始为蜡，蜡也者，索也。岁十二月，合聚万物而索饗之也" — "The Son of
  Heaven performs the Eightfold Year-End Sacrifice. Yi Qizhi was the first to institute
  the year-end sacrifice. The sacrifice is a searching-out (索); in the twelfth month
  of the year, all things are gathered together and searched out to receive the offering."


  The eight elements of this sacrifice encompass a complete vision of agricultural
  thanksgiving:


  1. **Main sacrifice to the First Harvester (主先啬)** — honoring the spirit of grain''s
  origin

  2. **Sacrifice to the Minister of Agriculture (祭司啬)** — honoring the officials who
  manage cultivation

  3. **Sacrifice to the hundred grains (祭百种)** — thanking all varieties of grain

  4. **Sacrifice to the farmer (饗农)** — honoring the peasants who work the land

  5. **Sacrifice at the field markers (及郵表畷)** — honoring the boundary markers and
  surveying officials

  6. **Sacrifice to birds and beasts (禽獸)** — acknowledging the creatures that coexist
  with agriculture


  The text emphasizes that this ceremony represents "仁之至、義之盡" — the ultimate expression
  of benevolence and righteousness. The ancients believed that all who served the
  agricultural cause deserved remembrance and reciprocation.


  A distinctive feature is the welcoming of the cat (迎貓), because it devours field
  mice that would destroy crops, and the tiger (迎虎), because it consumes wild boars
  that ravage the fields. These animals are received and sacrificed to with gratitude.


  The ceremony also includes prayers for natural harmony: "土反其宅" (May the soil return
  to its proper place), "水歸其壑" (May water flow to its channels), "昆蟲毋作" (May no insects
  arise), "草木歸其澤" (May vegetation grow in its appropriate marshy places).


  The "Grass Hat Speech" (草笠而至) delivered by the chief of the net-catchers (大羅氏) warns
  the assembled feudal lords: "好田好女者亡其國" — "Those who love hunting and women will
  destroy their own states."


  Post-ceremony, the text states: "既蜡而收，民息已" — "After the year-end sacrifice, the
  people rest." This marks the agricultural year''s true conclusion, with no further
  ceremonial duties until the new cycle begins.

**大蜡八**乃《禮記·郊特牲》所載天子年終蜡祭之禮。伊耆氏始創此制，「蜡」者，「索」也，謂歲十二月合聚萬物而索求饗祭之。

八蜡之祭，包舉萬端：
- **主先啬**而祭司啬：先啬者，百穀之始生；司啬者，農官之掌播種者。
- **祭百種**：以報百穀之功，感謝農作物多樣性之恩澤。
- **饗農及郵表畷**：慰問農夫及田間驛傳、阡陌之官。
- **祭禽獸**：仁之至也，義之盡也。古之君子，使必報之。

迎猫祭祀，因其食田鼠；迎虎祭祀，因其食田豕。此皆見古人順應生態、防護農田之意。

蜡祭之服，黃衣黃冠，息田夫也。草笠而至，尊野服。大羅氏乃天子掌鳥獸之官，諸侯貢屬焉。

蜡祭又有祈報之辭：「土反其宅，水歸其壑，昆蟲毋作，草木歸其澤。」此祈求風調雨順、五穀豐登也。

既蜡而收，民息已。蜡祭既畢，君子不興功。此歲終休息之義也。

八蜡所以記四方。四方年不順成，八蜡不通，以謹民財；順成之方，其蜡乃通，以移民也。
