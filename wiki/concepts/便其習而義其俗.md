content_legacy: '## English


  This concept captures Mozi''s critique of customs that are merely habituated practices
  rather than genuine expressions of righteousness. Mozi argues that some practices
  persist simply because they are customary, not because they embody true moral principles.


  Mozi illustrates this with three striking examples of funeral customs from distant
  lands: the people east of Yue who eat their firstborn sons; the people south of
  Chu who expose the bodies of the deceased; and the people west of Qin who cremate
  their dead. He notes that those who practice these customs claim they are acting
  righteously, "carrying out what has been passed down." But Mozi insists this is
  merely "便利其習俗而謂之義" (conveniently following their customs and calling it righteousness).


  The critique applies directly to elaborate funeral and mourning practices: those
  who observe them claim to be following the ancients, but they are simply following
  local customs. True righteousness requires examining whether practices genuinely
  benefit the world, not merely accepting them because they are traditional. This
  argument precedes Mozi''s discussion of proper burial as an alternative to both
  exotic customs and local traditions that lack rational justification.

便其習而義其俗，乃墨子批判厚葬久喪者之論理也。墨子以為，當世之君子，言必稱堯舜禹湯文武之道，然行則相非，皆所謂「便其習而義其俗」者耳。

墨子舉三事以為說：其一，越之東有輆沐之國，其長子生則解而食之，謂之宜弟；其二，楚之南有炎人之國，其親戚死則朽其肉而棄之；其三，秦之西有儀渠之國，其親戚死則聚柴薪而焚之。此三國者，皆以為俗，而謂之孝子之行。

夫以越人之食子為孝，楚人之棄肉為孝，秦人之焚尸為孝，是豈真孝道哉？不過便其習俗而稱之義耳。

墨子之言，以諷當時之厚葬久喪者：彼亦自謂循堯舜之道，而不知其實便其俗也已。真正之義，當視其實之可否，不當問其俗之從違。此墨子[[義-儒學-之]]之要旨也。
