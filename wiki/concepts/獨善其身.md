content_legacy: '## English


  **"Personally Cultivate Oneself"** (獨善其身) appears in the famous paired formulation
  with "benefiting all under heaven when successful" (達則兼善天下) in Mencius 7A:9:


  > "When poor, personally cultivate oneself; when successful, benefit all under heaven."


  This teaching addresses the Confucian response to circumstances of fortune and misfortune.
  When theWay cannot be realized in the world—when one lacks position, resources,
  or opportunity—the proper response is not to abandon virtue or resent society. Instead,
  one maintains personal cultivation, keeping the lamp of moral knowledge burning
  privately. This is not resignation but active preservation of one''s moral substance.


  The complementary teaching, "when successful, benefit all under heaven," assumes
  that those who achieve influence have the responsibility to extend their virtue
  outward. Personal cultivation is preparation for public service, not an end in itself.
  The truly virtuous person waits neither idly nor complacently; even in obscurity,
  one studies, reflects, and prepares.


  This pair of teachings reflects Mencius''s understanding of the relation between
  self-cultivation and world-order. The individual and society exist in mutual dependence.
  When circumstances allow, the cultivated person transforms the world; when they
  do not, the person remains ready, contributing through example what cannot be achieved
  through action.


  The passage comes from Mencius''s conversation with Song Goujian, where he teaches
  the attitude of the cultivated person: even without recognition, one remains morally
  consistent. This is the "囂囂" (tranquil acceptance) that characterizes the true junzi
  in times of adversity, a state of inner contentment uncorrupted by external judgment.
  [[修身]] and [[齊家]] provide the foundation for such composure, while [[誠]] maintains
  it through all circumstances.

  「独善其身」は『孟子』尽心上第九章に出る有名な対句の一部であり、「成功すれば天下を兼善する」と並ぶ。


  「困窮すれば独り身を善くし、達すれば天下を兼善す。」


  この言葉は、運命のよしあしに対する儒家の态度を示している。道の天下实行が進まないとき——地位も、资源も、機会もないとき——正しい反応は美徳を捨てたり、社会を恨んだりはしない。代わりに、个人的修养を維持し、道徳の灯を保ち続ける。これは諦めではなく、道徳的本質の能动的保存である。

「獨善其身」出自《孟子·盡心上》第九章，與「達則兼善天下」相對而言：

「窮則獨善其身；達則兼善天下。」

此語陳述儒家對窮達之際的態度。當道不行於天下——無位、無財、無機——君子不怨天、不尤人，不以為己道已終。乃退而修省，涵養德性，以待時機。此非消極退縮，乃是積蓄力量，保持靈台清明。

「達則兼善天下」謂既有功成名遂之日，當以所學澤加於民，非獨善其身而已矣。蓋修身非修身之目的，修身乃為經世致用之準備。君子之學，學以為人，非以自私。故雖在韋布，必學不厭、誨不倦，修持以待。

此對舉之說，反映孟子對修身與治世關係之理解。個人與社會相互依存：時機既至，修成之人以德化天下；時機未至，則以身教補口說之不足。
