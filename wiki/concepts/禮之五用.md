content_legacy: '## English


  **The Five Functions of Ritual** (禮之五用) from the *Book of Rites: Explanation of
  the Classics* (禮記·經解) outlines how ritual occasions structure and clarify the five
  fundamental relationships of human society.


  Confucius teaches that each major ritual serves a specific educational and ordering
  function:


  1. **朝覲之禮** (Court and Tribute Rituals): These clarify the duty between ruler and
  minister (君臣之義)

  2. **聘問之禮** (Diplomatic Visit Rituals): These foster mutual respect among feudal
  lords (諸侯相尊敬)

  3. **喪祭之禮** (Funeral and Sacrificial Rituals): These express the gratitude and duty
  of ministers and children toward their superiors (臣子之恩)

  4. **鄉飲酒之禮** (Village Assembly and Drinking Rituals): These establish the proper
  order between elders and younger (長幼之序)

  5. **昏姻之禮** (Wedding Rituals): These distinguish between men and women (男女之別)


  The passage emphasizes that ritual prevents disorder (禮禁亂之所由生), just as a dam controls
  flooding. When old rituals are considered useless and discarded, chaos follows:
  marriage rituals abandoned leads to marital suffering and licentiousness; village
  drinking rituals abandoned leads to loss of generational order and increased litigation;
  funeral rituals abandoned leads to declining filial gratitude and more disregard
  for the dead; diplomatic rituals abandoned leads to ruler-minister relations collapsing
  and vassal states'' malfeasance.


  Thus, ritual functions not merely as ceremony but as the foundational architecture
  of civilized society.'
