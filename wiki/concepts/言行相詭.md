content_legacy: '## English


  *Mozi·Lu Wen* presents a dialogue on judging character through examining intention
  and action together. When the Lord of Lu asked which of his two sons should be heir—one
  who loved learning, one who distributed wealth—Mozi replied: "It cannot yet be known.
  Perhaps their motives for these actions differ." He elaborated with metaphors: "A
  crab''s posture of deference is not for the fish''s benefit; setting out traps for
  rats with insects is not from affection for them. I wish the lord would examine
  both the intention (志) and the accomplishment (功) together."


  This principle—that one must judge people by unifying observation of their purpose
  and its result—appears in other classical sources with the phrase [[言行相詭]] or [[合志功而觀]].

《墨子·魯問》載魯君問其二子孰可以為太子：「一人者好學，一人者好分人財。」子墨子曰：「未可知也，或所為賞與為是也。魡者之恭，非為魚賜也；餌鼠以蟲，非愛之也。吾願主君之合其志功而觀焉。」

此節闡明觀人之要：在合[[志功而觀]]。魡者之恭，非愛魚也，乃為求魚；餌鼠以蟲，非愛鼠也，乃為得鼠。學者好學，或為邀賞；分人財者，或為求名。不可但觀其行，必察其志；亦不可但聽其言，必考其功。[[言行相詭]]之事，世多有之，故聖人貴「合志功而觀」也。
