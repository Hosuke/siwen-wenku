## English

The **Root-Branch Discrimination** (本末之辨) represents one of the most fundamental teachings in the 《大學》, establishing virtue (德) as the root and wealth (財) as the branch. The text declares explicitly: "德者本也，財者末也" — "Virtue is the root; wealth is the branches."

This distinction generates a chain of causal relationships: "有德此有人，有人此有土，有土此有財，有財此有用" — "With virtue, you gain people; with people, you gain territory; with territory, you gain wealth; with wealth, you have the means for use."

**Three Corollaries of This Principle:**

1. **On Governance**: A ruler who prioritizes virtue will attract devoted subjects; one who prioritizes wealth will engender greed and disaffection. "是故君子先慎乎德" — "Therefore the superior person first takes caution regarding virtue."

2. **On Wealth and People**: "財聚則民散，財散則民聚" — "When wealth is hoarded, the people scatter; when wealth is distributed, the people gather." The accumulation of wealth by the ruler corresponds to the dispersal of popular support.

3. **On the Moral Economy**: The text warns that "外本內末，爭民施奪" — "When the root is placed outside and the branch within, there will be mutual plunder among the people." Prioritizing material wealth over virtue corrupts the social order.

The teaching further warns that wealth obtained through immoral means (貨悖而入者) will likewise be lost immorally (亦悖而出). This principle also underlies the distinction between "仁者以財發身，不仁者以身發財" — "The benevolent use wealth to develop their character; the non-benevolent sacrifice their character to acquire wealth."
