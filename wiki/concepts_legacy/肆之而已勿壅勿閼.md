## English

**"Abandon All Restraint, Block Nothing"** (肆之而已，勿壅勿閼) is the life-nourishing doctrine that **Guan Yiwu** (管夷吾, i.e., Guan Gong) teaches to **Yan Ying** (晏平仲) in the *Liezi* chapter on Yang Zhu. This maxim prescribes complete freedom from self-imposed or socially imposed constraints on the senses, desires, and bodily inclinations.

When Yan Ying asks what exactly "abandoning all restraint" means, Guan Yiwu enumerates six domains of unchecked indulgence:

1. **The ears** may hear whatever sounds they desire.
2. **The eyes** may gaze upon whatever beauty they desire.
3. **The nose** may approach whatever fragrance (e.g., pepper or orchids) it desires.
4. **The mouth** may speak whatever it wishes, including right and wrong.
5. **The body** may seek whatever physical comfort it desires.
6. **The will** (意) may act according to its inclinations without inhibition.

Guan Yiwu then defines what "blocking" (壅閼) each sense actually means: to prevent the ears from hearing music is to "block hearing"; to prevent the eyes from seeing beautiful colors is to "block vision"; to prevent the nose from smelling orchids is to "block the sense of smell"; to prevent the mouth from speaking is to "block wisdom"; to prevent the body from comfort is to "block ease"; to prevent the will from acting freely is to "block nature." These blockages are termed "the lords of harm and cruelty"
