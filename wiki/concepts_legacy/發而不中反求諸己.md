## English

"發而不中，反求諸己" (releasing the arrow without hitting, turn back to examine oneself) is the core moral principle of the archery rite, encapsulating the ritual's significance as a method of self-cultivation. This concept asserts that when one fails to hit the target, one should not resent those who performed better, but should instead seek the cause within oneself.

The passage from *The Meaning of the Archery Rite* states: "射者，仁之道也。射求正諸己，己正而後發，發而不中，則不怨勝己者，反求諸己而已矣" — Archery is the way of benevolence. Archery seeks to correct oneself; once oneself is corrected, then one releases [the arrow]. If, upon releasing, one does not hit, one should not resent those who surpassed oneself, but simply turn to examine oneself.

This principle operates on multiple levels. First, as a **technical matter** (技術層面), archery requires the shooter to be internally aligned before releasing. The text emphasizes: "內志正，外體直，然後持弓矢審固；持弓矢審固，然後可以言中" — The inner will must be correct, the outer body straight; only then can one hold the bow firmly; only with firm holding can one speak of hitting the target. If the inner state is disordered, even perfect technical form cannot guarantee success.

Second, as a **moral matter** (德行層面), failure provides an opportunity for self-reflection rather than recrimination. Rather than blaming external factors or envying others' success, the archer accepts responsibility for the miss and investigates his own shortcomings. This self-examination (反求諸己) is the same principle articulated in the *Great Learning* (大學): "君子有諸己而後求諸人，無諸己而後非諸人" — The superior person first has the virtue in himself before demanding it of others; first exempts himself from the fault before blaming others for it.

Third, as a **political matter** (治道層面), this principle undergirds the accountability system of the archery selection. When lords failed to hit the target, they accepted the consequence of territorial reduction without complaint, recognizing their own failure.
