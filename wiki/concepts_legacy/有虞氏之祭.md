## English

The *Book of Rites* provides a fascinating comparative analysis of sacrificial practices across the Three Dynasties, revealing how each era emphasized different sensory dimensions in their communion with the spiritual world.

**Yu Dynasty: Prioritizing Qi (氣)**

The Yu Dynasty approach was characterized by the use of raw, vital energy in sacrifice. According to the text, "有虞氏之祭也，尚用氣" — the people of Yu esteemed the use of qi. Their offerings included **血腥爓祭** — raw blood, meat, and partially cooked sacrifices, all designed to transmit vital energy directly to the spirits. The emphasis was on the raw, essential power of life itself.

**Shang Dynasty: Prioritizing Sound (聲)**

The Shang people "尚聲" — esteemed sound. Before any sacrifice could begin, they would perform a thorough purification of their instruments: "臭味未成，滌蕩其聲" — the sounds would be cleansed and prepared. They would play music three times before proceeding to welcome the sacrificial victim. The text explains that "聲音之號，所以詔告於天地之間也" — musical sounds served as the announcement that summoned the spirits between heaven and earth.

**Zhou Dynasty: Prioritizing Scent (臭)**

The Zhou people "尚臭" — esteemed scent. Their sacrificial approach focused on the **灌用鬯臭** — pouring libations of aromatic wine (鬯) mixed with郁 (wormwood), producing a powerful fragrance that "陰達於淵泉" — penetrated deeply into the underworld springs. The text describes how "既灌，然後迎牲，致陰氣也" — after the libation, they would welcome the victim, thereby contacting the yin forces below.

**Complementary Unity**

The three approaches — qi, sound, and scent — should not be seen as contradictory but as complementary expressions of the same spiritual truth. Each dynasty developed its own sensory language for communicating with the unseen world:

- **Blood and flesh** (qi) represent life substance
- **Music and voice** (sound) represent spiritual announcement
- **Fragrance and wine** (scent) represent penetrating connection

The final passage emphasizes the complete ritual picture: "祭有祈焉，有報焉，有由辟焉" — sacrifice includes prayers for blessings, expressions of gratitude, and rituals of dispelling misfortune. This triadic structure underlies all three dynastic approaches.

## 中文

《禮記·郊特牲》詳論三代祭祀之異，曰：「有虞氏之祭也，尚用氣；血腥爓祭，用氣也。殷人尚聲，臭味未成，滌蕩其聲；樂三闋，然後出迎牲。周人尚臭，灌用鬯臭，郁合鬯。」

**虞氏尚氣**

有虞氏之祭，以氣為尚。所用者血腥爓祭——生血、生肉、半熟之肉，皆所以用氣以事神也。氣者，生之始也；得其氣，斯通於神矣。

**殷人尚聲**

殷人尚聲，音樂為先。祭祀之先，必滌蕩其聲，使音清而純。樂三闋，乃出迎牲。聲音之號，所以詔告於天地之間也。此以音聲達於神明之義也。

**周人尚臭**

周人尚臭，香氣為重。灌用鬯臭，郁合鬯——以郁金草合鬯酒，芬芳四達，其氣陰達淵泉。既灌，然後迎牲，致陰氣也。

三者之異，各有所重，然皆通於神、明於道者也。祭祀之時，或求焉，或報焉，或由辟焉。此三代相承之大義也。

## 日本語

『禮記·郊特牲』は三朝の祭祀の異なるアプローチを比較する：

**有虞の世：気を尊ぶ**

有虞の祭祀は「氣」（き）を尊んだ。生の血と肉による「血腥爓祭」を行い、 жизненную энергию 直接神に屆けた。生の實質が最も神聖な形態であった。

**殷の世：音を尊ぶ**

殷人は「聲」（こえ）を尊んだ。祭祀の前に樂器を清め、三度はoplayし、牲を迎える。音聲の響きが天地間の靈を呼び寄せた。

**周の世：臭いを尊ぶ**

周人は「臭」（におい）を尊んだ。鬯酒（香りを添えた酒）を注ぐ「灌」の儀で、其の香りが地下の淵泉に浸透し、幽界との交通を可能にした。

三つのアプローチは矛盾ではなく、補完的なものである。各自が感覺器官を通じて神との絆を深める独自の方法を開拓した。
