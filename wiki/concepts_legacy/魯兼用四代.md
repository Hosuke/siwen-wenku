## English

The concept of "Lu comprehensively using the Four Dynasties" (魯兼用四代) emerges from the *Liji's* detailed enumeration of ritual elements employed by the state of Lu, which synthesizes practices from the Youyu (有虞氏), Xia (夏后氏), Shang (殷), and Zhou (周) dynasties. This comprehensive adoption rendered Lu unique among all vassal states and became the foundation for its claim to represent orthodox Zhou ritual traditions.

The *Liji* explicitly states: "All the garments, vessels, and offices of the Four Dynasties—Lu used them all. Therefore Lu possesses the rites of the King, and this has been transmitted through the world for a long time" (凡四代之服、器、官，魯兼用之。是故，魯，王禮也，天下傳之久矣).

This passage from the *Mingtang Wei* chapter represents a crucial theoretical statement about the nature of ritual transmission. Lu, as the enfeoffment of the Duke of Zhou, was granted the extraordinary privilege of using rites proper to the Son of Heaven himself. The text enumerates numerous specific elements from each dynasty:

**Ritual Vessels (尊俎豆):** Youyu had the Tai (泰) vessel; Xia used the Mountain Lei (山罍); Shang employed the Zhu (著); Zhou favored the Xi Xiang (犧象). For sacrificial stands (俎), Youyu used Kuan (梡), Xia used Jue (嶡), Shang used Ju (椇), and Zhou used Fang Zu (房俎). For cups and dishes (爵琖), Youyu had the Xian (琖), Shang used the Jia (斝), and Zhou employed the Jue (爵).

**Musical Instruments:** The four generations used: Pigu (土鼓), Fubo (拊搏), Jade Chimes (玉磬), large qin and se, medium and small qin. The *Liji* also describes specific drums: Youyu had foot drums (足鼓), Shang used pillar drums (楹鼓), and Zhou employed suspended drums (縣鼓).

**Official Systems:** Youyu had fifty officials; Xia had one hundred; Shang doubled this to two hundred; Zhou further expanded to three hundred.

**Ritual Offerings:** Each dynasty sacrificed different organs: Youyu offered the head (祭首), Xia the heart (祭心), Shang the liver (祭肝), and Zhou the lungs (祭肺). Their preferred beverages also varied: Youyu favored clear water (尚明水), Shang preferred sweet wine (尚醴), and Zhou favored spirits (尚酒).

This comprehensive synthesis demonstrates that Zhou ritual culture was not a complete break from earlier traditions but represented an inclusive synthesis that preserved and transformed the achievements of previous dynasties. Lu's unique position as the repository of this comprehensive tradition explains its central role in the development of Confucian ritual thought.
